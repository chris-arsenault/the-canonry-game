# The Shelf Yields a Tome

Status: coverage-focused workshop candidate

## Purpose

This generator exists to improve first-pass subkind coverage for quieter, discovery-shaped events. The current starter set already has catastrophe, frontier consolidation, civic formalization, and sanctuary revelation. What it lacks is a more scholarly or archival emergence that can still create a real connected bundle.

## Summary

- Target kind: intentionally flexible for now
- Narrative event: a named shelf becomes legible through the recovery of a tome, and the discovery may stabilize into a rookery and scholar

## Trigger shape

Keep this broad for now. The point of this draft is coverage and event shape, not precise semantic authoring.

- conditions that make buried record, preserved memory, or recovered language plausible
- some combination of pristiness, holding, echo, or pressure that would let a shelf disclose something authored rather than merely physical
- enough significance that the shelf and the recovered object both become named

We can decide later whether the cleanest target is `Region`, `Artifact`, `Settlement`, or even `Penguin`.

## Created entities

- `Artifact` `tome`
- `Region` `shelf`
- optional `Settlement` `rookery`
- optional `Penguin` `scholar`

## Created relationships

- tome tied to the new shelf as native, recovered, or revealed there
- optional rookery tied to the shelf as the site that forms around the discovery
- optional scholar tied to the rookery or tome as custodian, interpreter, or claimant
- if both rookery and scholar are present, they should make the discovery socially legible rather than leaving it as a lone object

Exact relationship choices can wait until the next semantic pass.

## Non-creation consequences

- the world gains a more archival form of revelation, not just rupture or consolidation
- a named shelf can become a site of study, custody, copying, pilgrimage, or dispute
- the recovered tome gives us a stronger textual expression for The Ice Remembers than seals and relics alone

## Why this belongs in the starter expansion set

- It improves subkind coverage directly: `tome`, `shelf`, `rookery`, and `scholar`
- It adds a quieter but still generator-shaped event
- It expands artifact vocabulary toward text and memory rather than only warding and relics
- It helps the world feel like it contains records and interpreters, not only rulers, wardens, and cults

## Open questions

- Should the tome feel sacred, administrative, prophetic, cartographic, or forbidden?
- Is the rookery a scholarly outpost, a copying-house, a watch-site, or just the first named habitation to gather around the shelf?
- Should the scholar be optional in the base version, or is the recovered text too inert without a named reader?
- Does the shelf want to read as exposed memory, preserved archive, or dangerous disclosure?
