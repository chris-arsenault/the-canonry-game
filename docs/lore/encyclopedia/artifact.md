# Artifact

*Archivist's register: material entry, general.*

An artifact is an object that has outlived the purpose for which it was made, or
an object whose purpose has not yet admitted itself. The Berg produces many
tools, charms, ledgers, masks, bells, picks, seals, charts, rings, and shards.
Only a few acquire enough memory, fear, debt, or use to be catalogued as
artifacts.

The useful distinction is not whether an artifact is magical. On the Berg,
objects become involved with magic by being handled near enough to cost. The
useful distinction is what kind of attention the object has learned to demand.

## Common Sorts

- **Relics** are objects kept because the dead still press through them.
- **Instruments** extend perception, heat, craft, navigation, or judgment.
- **Weapons** are objects whose history can be spent directly against a body,
  barrier, tunnel, pod, or settlement.
- **Tomes** hold records that do not remain passive under reading.
- **Seals** close doors, debts, tunnels, names, or agreements. A broken seal is
  often more active than an intact one.

These categories are habits of cataloguing, not laws of nature. The Radiant Ring
has been relic, instrument, weapon, and burden, depending on which exhausted
flipper held it. The Hollows Chart is a map until it begins drawing routes that
the mapper never walked.

## How Artifacts Behave In Story

Artifacts move through custody more than through walking. They are found in
vaults, traded in ledgers, hidden in shrines, stolen across Midway Shelf, sunk
below the ice, recovered from drowned ledges, or left in ruins because every
living witness understands what picking them up would require.

They do not need to grant power cleanly. The better question is always: what
does the object make easier, and what does that ease teach the bearer to ignore?
Aurora-light artifacts often cost clarity. Fire-worked objects often cost flesh.
Pressure-depth relics often cost nearness to others. Before Times objects may
refuse all three bargains and impose a fourth.

## Chronicle Use

Use artifacts when a record needs a portable pressure: a reason for factions to
move, a memory that will not stay buried, a tool whose usefulness is also its
argument. Do not make every artifact a prize. Many are liabilities that survive
because no one has found a way to be rid of them.
