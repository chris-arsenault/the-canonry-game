# Ability

*Archivist's register: kind entry, general.*

An ability is a transferable competence — a combat form, a magical rite, a craft
of fire and metal — preserved in a physical carrier and held in living practice.
It is the book that teaches a stance, the marking inked into a flipper, the tool
whose use is the lesson. The carrier is real. The ability is what remains when a
competent reader picks the carrier up and does not put it down.

Penguins disagree about where the ability lives. The Stack records the carrier,
counts witnesses to the practice, and treats the chain of teaching as a public
record. The Shelf hides the carrier and trusts only the practitioner. The orca,
when they engage with penguin abilities at all, recognize that a thing held by
many is not the same as a thing held by one and refuse the comparison.

## Common Sorts

- **Techniques** are bodily forms — the hunting stance, the breath-count of an
  ice-cutter, the footwork by which a warden survives a corridor she cannot see
  the end of. They cost flesh in the learning and flesh again in the keeping.
- **Rites** are ritual operations: invocations, observances, sacrifices that
  produce results their participants would not have produced otherwise. They
  cost what their tradition extracts. The three fires each name their bargain
  honestly.
- **Crafts** are technologies: ice-reading, fire-core handling, lens grinding,
  the practical sciences that succeed or fail by reproducibility. They cost
  apprenticeship measured in years, and the apprenticeship is part of the craft.

These categories blur. A craft can carry a rite. A technique can become a craft
when its conditions are written down clearly enough to teach a stranger.

## Lineage

An ability remembers its teachers. The chain from originator to current
practitioner is preserved in the practice itself — stance, vocabulary, breath
count, the small misalignments that mark which line a student descends from. A
careful reader can tell whether a forge-master was taught by Coastlands tradition
or Heartlands tradition without the master saying anything. Some traditions
treat lineage as a credential. Some treat it as a liability. The Communion treats
it as both, sometimes in the same audit.

Lineage is not safety. A teacher can be silenced. A chain can be cut. An ability
whose every living practitioner dies will fade from the world even if the carrier
survives. The carrier becomes a ledger of an absence. The Communion of the
Clever Ice Age learned this when methods of ice-reading were dismantled with
the same administrative care that had once preserved them. The books still
exist. The reading does not.

## Costs

Every ability extracts what its tradition extracts. An Aurora method costs
clarity of self. A Nightshelf craft costs flesh. An orca song costs interior
distance. A practitioner who carries multiple abilities of incompatible
traditions can carry contradiction in their body for the rest of their life.
Some of them do.

## Chronicle Use

Use abilities when a story needs a transmissible competence whose lineage
matters. Do not collapse them into the carrier they ride in. The book is not the
rite. The marking is not the form. When an ability moves between hands, the
question to ask is not what was taken but what was taught, and by whom, and at
what cost to which side.
