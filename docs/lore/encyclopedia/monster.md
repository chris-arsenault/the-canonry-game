# Monster

*Archivist's register: kind entry, general.*

A monster is a peril that has acquired a name and a shape. It is not yet free.
It is bound — by a seal, by a current, by a doctrine, by the geometry of the
corridor that contains it, by sustained sacrifice, by the simple cold of the
deep against pressure — and the binding holds, mostly, often. The records do
not promise more.

The category is unstable on purpose. Some monsters were once living things.
Some were never. Some are deep ice corruption made local and named. Some are
Before Times residue that has acquired a shape and an appetite. The thread that
connects them is that the world treats them as bound, and the binding is the
thing the chronicle is keeping watch on.

## Common Sorts

- **Leviathans** are large bodies, mostly aquatic, whose containment is their
  environment as much as any seal. The deep below the Corpse Current may hold
  one. There may be others. The chroniclers have learned not to commit to
  counts.
- **Wraiths** are bound presences without bodies, attached to places, objects,
  or named lineages. Their containment is ritual, attention, and the silence of
  those who could speak.
- **Beasts** are physical and aggressive: hunters, scavengers, warped fauna.
  Their containment is most often distance, terrain, and the work of patrols
  who prefer their own names to remain quiet.
- **Revenants** are returns: bodies that did not stay still, oaths that did not
  stay sealed, broken bonds that re-formed under pressure. Their containment is
  the discipline that made the original stillness possible.

## Containment

A monster is well-bound when the wards hold, the offerings continue, the
corridor that frames it is maintained, and no faction has begun debating
whether the cost of containment is exceeding the cost of release. A monster is
poorly bound when one of those things has begun to fail.

The conditions that loosen a binding are familiar from deep ice corruption:
containment gaps, attention gaps, secrecy used as a substitute for repair,
extraction without acknowledgement of what is being extracted. The corruption
finds available space. So does the named peril.

A monster does not need to escape to harm the world. A poorly-bound peril can
bleed dread into a corridor, hum below a working level, stain the ice across an
unwatched ward, whisper to a bearer of a useful technique until the technique
starts to drift. The visible event is rarely the worst event.

## Cost Of Knowing

To know a monster is to be implicated. The Nightshelf understands this clearly
enough that the Unspoken treats certain bindings as topics of structural
silence. The Stack records what it dares and forgets what it cannot afford to
register. The orca approach the question with theological patience and
operational care, and what they do with their understanding is not always
available to penguin record.

## Chronicle Use

Use monsters as named pressures. The peril should have a binding, the binding
should have a cost, and the cost should be visible in the lives of those
holding the line. Do not solve a monster cleanly. Even a destroyed monster
leaves a corridor changed, a doctrine spent, and a chronicler unwilling to
write what they saw.
