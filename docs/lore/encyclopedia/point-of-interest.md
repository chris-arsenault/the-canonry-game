# Point of Interest

*Archivist's register: kind entry, general.*

A point of interest is a natural feature on the Berg or in the surrounding
waters that has acquired a name. A vent that warms a corridor through three
colonies' worth of failed insulation. A peak whose silhouette is the eastern
survey's only honest landmark. A drift where krill bloom thick enough to fund a
winter. A shoal whose contour every reefhold knows by sound.

A point of interest is not a settlement. Nothing was built. It is not a region;
the region is what surrounds it, owns it, fights over it, and sometimes forgets
it. It is the place itself, named because the naming was inevitable.

## Common Sorts

- **Vents** are openings where heat reaches surface or near-surface. The Berg
  has many. The named ones are those whose heat survived a season under
  pressure of need.
- **Peaks** are summits, spires, and ridges whose visibility serves navigation,
  prayer, or claim. A peak that no one needs to see is a feature. A peak that
  several patrols use to orient is a point.
- **Blooms** are seasonal but reliable concentrations of krill, plankton, or
  other life. The bloom is named for the place, not the season; the place
  returns even when the season fails.
- **Shoals** are shallows, ridges, or reef-edges where current and depth combine
  to make the same fishing possible across many years.

## What A Place Does

A point of interest changes life around it without doing anything in
particular. A vent makes a corridor habitable that would otherwise empty. A
peak gives a courier a horizon to navigate against. A bloom feeds a colony
that would otherwise migrate. A shoal absorbs a fishing pressure that would
otherwise be displaced inland.

The Stack records points and uses them as anchors for charters and surveys. The
Shelf knows them and prefers not to write them down. Orca pods know the
maritime points by current and silence and would not call them points; they
would say the bloom is a hunting fact and the vent is a place where the cold
complains.

## Pristine And Spent

A point can be drained. A bloom over-fished collapses. A vent over-tapped
dries. A shoal worked through three winters becomes a shoal worked through
three winters and not the same shoal. The Berg restores some of this and not
all of it.

Treacherous approaches are points whose value is undeniable and whose access
kills. A vent in unstable rock. A peak whose face calves. A bloom in waters orca
pods claim. The point survives. Those who reach it sometimes do not.

## Chronicle Use

Use points of interest when a region needs more than terrain. They give a
faction a reason to send a courier; they give a settlement a reason to
overextend; they give a chronicler a place to set a small disaster the
surrounding region cannot quite explain. A point's name should hint at what
reaching it costs.
