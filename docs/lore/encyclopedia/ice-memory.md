# Ice-Memory

*Archivist's register: metaphysical entry.*

The ice-memory is the Berg's record of what happens on and within it. It is not
water made sentimental. It is not a court, not a god, not a parent, and not a
guarantee that justice will follow truth. It remembers triumph and murder with
the same patience.

Events leave impressions. Some are faint enough to fade within a life. Some
remain for millennia. Violent death, strong feeling, repeated ritual, many
witnesses, and magical use press more deeply. The deepest layers contain
impressions older than penguins and shaped by minds that may not have had names.

## Reading

Ice-readers do not fetch perfect facts from a shelf. They enter a state in which
the impression can be perceived, and perception remains personal. One reader may
hear a name. Another may taste fear. A third may see the angle of a tool-mark
and nothing else. The ice records objectively. Penguins read subjectively.

Aurora courts admit ice-testimony because they have little better and because
public procedure demands a witness. The Nightshelf distrusts the same testimony
because anything recorded can become leverage. Orca dismiss the entire matter
until the deep ice records a broken pod-bond at the same frequency as a broken
penguin oath, at which point dismissal becomes a social convenience rather than
an argument.

## Costs

Aurora-light lets a reader ask after memory, distance, and pattern. The cost is
clarity of self. Use it too long and a penguin's private memories become crowded
with other lives. Some advanced readers answer to names that were never theirs
because the distinction has grown thin.

The ice may also hold anticipatory echoes: impressions that feel like what will
be, what might be, or what is happening elsewhere. Mechanists call these errors.
Animists call them evidence. Archivists call them dangerous footnotes and keep
reading.

## Chronicle Use

Use ice-memory to preserve ambiguity with evidence, not to erase ambiguity. It
can confirm that something happened while leaving motive, meaning, and future
consequence painfully open.
