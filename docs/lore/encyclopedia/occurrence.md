# Occurrence

*Archivist's register: kind entry, general.*

An occurrence is an event large enough to be named while it is happening. A war
that has earned a name. A trade conference that everyone is traveling to. A
plague whose course is being charted in real time. A festival the rookeries are
preparing for and that the chroniclers have already begun to record.

An occurrence is not the past. It is happening. While it is happening, it pulls
entities toward itself: factions choose sides, settlements stage themselves
around it, penguins travel to be present, artifacts surface because the moment
has come for them to surface. When it ends — by exhaustion, by resolution, by
victory, by collapse — what is left is a memory, a chronicle entry, and an
altered world. The occurrence itself is gone.

## Common Sorts

- **Wars** organize violence around a doctrine, a territory, a debt, or an
  inheritance that did not transfer cleanly. They consume what they organize and
  produce a peace that contains the war's shape inside it.
- **Trade conferences** organize negotiation around a need, a route, or a
  contested allocation. They draw factions, bind ledgers, and create both the
  formal accord and the informal arrangements that the formal accord
  deliberately leaves unaddressed.
- **Plagues** organize loss around a vector, a corridor, or a habit. Some are
  biological. Some are not. The chronicle distinguishes them less reliably than
  the chroniclers would prefer.
- **Festivals** organize attention around a mourning, a memory, a season, an
  oath, or a relief. Even festivals of joy carry old griefs braided into them.
  The chronicle takes festivals seriously because the festival often shows what
  an era believes about itself before the era is willing to say so plainly.

## Momentum And Resolution

An occurrence has momentum. While participants gather, while the chronicle
widens, while resources flow toward it, the occurrence accelerates. Once
participants leave, attention thins, and the work that fed the occurrence
stops, momentum bleeds away. When it reaches zero the occurrence is over.

Endings are rarely clean. A war ends when the last battle stops, but the
chronicle of the war keeps writing for a generation. A festival ends when the
lanterns are extinguished, but the small obligations its presence created
continue to be discharged for years. A plague's last case is rarely the last
word a chronicler is willing to commit to.

## Effects On The World

An occurrence does not exist in a corner. While it runs, it shapes the
relationships of those caught in its gravity. Allies become opponents inside its
frame; opponents become temporary participants in its logistics; observers
become witnesses, and witness changes a penguin. When the occurrence resolves,
those changes remain. A faction that supported a festival is not the same
faction afterward. A settlement that survived a plague is not the settlement
that hosted it.

This is why occurrences are named. The Stack records them so that the changes
they leave can be referenced. The Shelf names them — sometimes — because what
cannot be named cannot be charged against. The orca recognize them as currents
in penguin politics that briefly become readable to outside observation.

## Chronicle Use

Use occurrences as named active context. They should pull characters toward
them; they should leave consequences when they end; their resolutions should
never be only one thing. A war's end is also a market crash, a generation's
grief, and a series of relationships that will not be the same.
