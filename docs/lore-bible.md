# Lore Bible: The Ice Remembers

*A denormalized reference for the world of Aurora Berg, compiled from the Canonry default project ("Penguin Colony World"). Every section is written to stand on its own; facts are repeated where context demands rather than cross-referenced.*

---

## 1. The Premise in One Paragraph

Aurora Berg is a continent-sized iceberg drifting through an endless frozen sea at the bottom of the world. Three sapient peoples share it: the **Aurora Stack** penguins on the sunlit surface, the **Nightshelf** penguins in the lightless tunnels beneath, and the **Orca** in the waters surrounding it. The ice itself is a vast, slow consciousness — the **ice-memory** — that records everything that happens on or in it. Events leave impressions; the dead persist as echoes; identity and history are literally frozen into the ground the penguins walk on. Beneath the waters, something two miles long lies on the abyssal floor, heart still beating once every several minutes. The civilizations here cannot leave — the Frozen Sea has no documented edge — so every conflict must eventually be settled with whoever is still standing when it's over.

## 2. The World: Aurora Berg ("The Berg")

### 2.1 Physical Character

Aurora Berg is not a single landmass. It is a complex arrangement of ice shelves, volcanic archipelagos, and a great central plateau called **The Stack**. It is supernatural in scale — large enough to contain mountains, caverns, and civilizations that have never seen its edges. It drifts under perpetual twilight with the aurora crowning it, pulsing with power even the oldest mystics do not fully understand.

The Berg knows only two true seasons — **The Endless Day** and **The Long Dark** — separated by two ruinous transitions, **The Thaw** and **The Freeze**.

### 2.2 Major Regions

**Aurora Stack (the sunlit face).** A vertical colony of crystalline spires carved into volcanic highlands, where ice-plate towers catch and channel aurora light through networks of prismatic lenses. Penguins here wear flowing white robes, speak in measured astronomical metaphors, and believe the aurora judges all actions. Tiers:
- **The Apex / Upper Reaches** — home to ice-seers and high auditors, closest to the aurora.
- **The Trade Terraces** — the great markets, dominated by the Icebound Exchange.
- **The Huddle Warrens** — dense communal residences where warmth is shared.
- **The Foundation Depths** — ancient tunnels carved by volcanic heat; workers who maintain vents; the Archive lives here.

**Nightfall Shelf (the shadowed interior).** A labyrinthine colony carved beneath the permanent ice shelf, lit by bioluminescent ice glyphs and fire-core braziers. Penguins here dress in charcoal wraps with ember-thread embroidery visible only in darkness, speak in coded proverbs, and trust silence over speech. Regions:
- **The Shroud Depths** — where the shelf meets the sea.
- **The Whisper Galleries** — acoustic chambers that carry secrets.
- **The Void Markets** — where forbidden things change hands; controlled by the Midnight Claws.
- **The Glow-Fissure** — a deep crack pulsing with cold blue-green light, depths forbidden.

**The Orca Territories (the open waters).** Defined not by structures but by claim. Orcas do not build; they mark territory with the bones of their kills.
- **The Hunting Currents** — migration routes of prey.
- **The Corpse Current** — a warm thermal anomaly where the dead god's blood still circulates, sacred to the Wake-Singers.
- **The Deep Trenches** — where elder orcas descend to die.
- **The Killing Shallows** — ambush zones near penguin colonies.

### 2.3 The Frozen Sea

The Frozen Sea surrounds The Berg in every direction. It is not an ocean in any useful sense — it is a boundary. Flat, featureless, endless. **No penguin has ever found its edge.** The longest documented expedition, by the Aurora cartographer Piien (twice-hatched), lasted forty-one days by krill-fed sea-bird sled; she turned back when her food math said she had to, and wrote in her journal: *"The sea does not end. I think perhaps it is not meant to."*

The Nightshelf believe the sea is a prison wall, keeping The Berg either contained or protected from something beyond. Orca dismiss the question — the sea is where they hunt; its size is irrelevant.

The sea's three states:
- **Endless Day** — open water, thick with krill, warm enough for blooms and cold enough to kill a penguin in minutes.
- **The transitions (Thaw/Freeze)** — most treacherous. Pressure ridges erupt overnight. Channels close by dusk. The ice sings constantly.
- **Long Dark** — frozen solid for miles from shore. Walkable. Claims most "Walking" deaths.

Shore-watch duty rotates every two weeks. Longer, and the watchers develop the "glass stare" — a thousand-yard focus on a horizon that offers nothing back. **Ferrymaster Owke** established this rotation after losing three watchers in a single Long Dark to the Walking.

### 2.4 The Only Home

Because the sea has no crossable edge, there is nowhere else. Every conflict must be resolved, every resource managed, every death absorbed. This is the foundational constraint of civilization on The Berg.

## 3. The Seasons

### 3.1 The Endless Day (Summer)

Four months of uninterrupted sunlight. Ice retreats, seas teem with krill, penguins gorge to prepare for winter. Time of festivals, mating, forgetting. But: melting ice reveals things that should stay buried (including Before Times artifacts), crevasses open, and orca grow bold.

### 3.2 The Long Dark (Winter)

Four months without sunrise. Surface temperatures drop below −60°C; wind chill pushes effective temperature to −100°C. Exposed flesh freezes in seconds. The sea freezes solid for miles.

During Long Dark:
- The boundary between the living world and the ice-memory grows thin. Ancestors speak more clearly. Visions come unbidden. Ice-chronicles sometimes record events out of temporal sequence.
- The **aurora** burns brighter than at any other time, as if compensating for the absent sun.
- **The Huddle** — Aurora Stack's mass communal survival practice: hundreds or thousands press together, rotating constantly between cold outer edge and warm center. Everyone takes equal turns; even the most mythic penguin rotates out. Individual identity partially dissolves. Many describe it as the closest they come to understanding the ice-memory.
- **The Deep Warrens** — Nightshelf retreats deepen; shadow-practices intensify; Shadow-Broker Nyla's power is said to peak.
- **The Dark Watchers** — some penguins report presences at the edge of perception, ancient things waking up.
- **The Walking** — a compulsion that seizes certain grief-stricken penguins to leave shelter and walk into the darkness. Some are found frozen with peaceful expressions. Some are never found. Some return weeks later, unable to explain.
- **Dark-born chicks** — those hatched during Long Dark are believed to have stronger connections to ice-memory, greater cold tolerance, and a certain grimness of disposition.
- **Suspended norms** — debts uncollected, crimes unpunished. This creates a strange atmosphere: tense but liberated. Things get said in the dark that are forbidden in light.
- **The Counting of Names** (Aurora) — public recitation of every penguin who died since the last Long Dark. Crowd responds *"Witnessed."* Bad years run for three days straight.

### 3.3 The Thaw (six weeks)

The sea ice breaks up in lurches. The **ice-break** is audible from miles — "The Berg screaming." Thaw casualty rate averages twelve per season. **The orca return** with Long Dark hunger; the first post-thaw fishing party is the most dangerous of the year. The first Flipper Accord tribunal session of the year is traditionally ugly — four months of grievances delivered by delegates fed on krill jerky and recycled arguments. Couriers call it "the screaming session."

### 3.4 The Freeze (six weeks)

Counted in remaining days. Quartermasters post food arithmetic on Trade Terrace boards — calories per penguin per day versus days remaining. When numbers run thin, the Huddle Warrens go quiet. The **Last Catch** is the final fishing expedition before the sea seals; it runs like a military operation. Some orca pods withdraw (a well-fed colony survives to be better hunting next Thaw); others see the Last Catch as the year's best hunting opportunity. **The Final Seal** is the day the last channel closes. There is no ceremony. Penguins go back to checking food stores. The silence after the Final Seal is the loudest thing on The Berg.

## 4. The Ice Remembers — The Metaphysics

*"The ice does not forget. The ice does not forgive. The ice simply... is. And within it, so are we."*

### 4.1 What the Ice-Memory Is

Not frozen water. A vast, slow medium that **records** everything that happens on or in the ice. Events leave impressions in the crystalline structure — not physical marks, but something else. Trained ice-readers perceive them as visual fragments, emotional residue, auditory echoes, or even ancestral contact.

It is:
- **Not a god** — it does not intervene, judge, or respond to prayer.
- **Not omniscient** — only records what happens on or in the ice.
- **Not prophetic** — it remembers the past. Whether it also remembers things that haven't happened yet is a question Quillen val'Foundation has been asked to stop raising at committee meetings.
- **Not benevolent or malevolent** — it records tragedy as readily as triumph.

### 4.2 The Consciousness Debate

Three scholarly camps:
- **Mechanists** — the ice is a recording medium. No more conscious than a wax cylinder.
- **Animists** — the ice possesses a vast, slow awareness. They cite surfaces cracking at significant moments and formations that seem to guide travelers.
- **Agnostics** — the distinction may not matter. Whether conscious or not, it behaves as if it remembers.

### 4.3 How It Works

**Recording.** Impressions form automatically, thresholded by emotional weight or physical impact. Factors that strengthen impressions: violent death, strong emotion (fear, love, rage, despair), magical activity, many witnesses, repetition.

**Persistence.** Strong impressions can last millennia. Weaker ones fade within decades or centuries. The deepest layers — miles down, compressed for millions of years — contain incomprehensible impressions from before penguins. Some impressions seem permanent.

**Anticipatory echoes.** Readers of the deep strata occasionally report echoes that feel anticipatory — not memories of what was, but resonances of what will be, or what might be, or what is happening elsewhere on The Berg at the same moment. Mechanists call these pattern-matching errors. Animists note that a conscious ice may not experience time the way penguins do. Quillen val'Foundation's private notes on the subject fill three volumes and conclude, unhelpfully, *"further reading required."*

**Reading.** A taught but innate-aptitude skill. Readers enter meditative states. The ice records objectively; perception remains subjective. The same impression may be read differently by different readers.

### 4.4 Applications

- **Historical research** — reconstructing major events; hearing the testimony of the dead.
- **Legal proceedings** — in Aurora courts, ice-testimony is admissible evidence. Not foolproof; readers can be biased.
- **Communication with the dead** — strong impressions retain enough personality for limited communication. The dead cannot learn or grow, but can share what they knew in life.
- **Warning systems** — experienced readers sometimes detect impressions that haven't fully formed yet. Not prophecy; pattern recognition at vast scale.

### 4.5 Ice-Memory and the Three Peoples

- **Aurora** — identity is accumulated; you are the sum of what the ice has witnessed you become. You can change who you are by performing different deeds. You cannot escape your past. Death is not erasure.
- **Nightshelf** — the ice-memory is a surveillance system to be exploited by those who know how to read it. Hence: secrecy, incomplete names, actions performed in shadow where the ice cannot see.
- **Orca** — do not believe in ice-memory at all. They live in water, not on ice. Whatever the ice records, it does not record them. You simply *are* what you *do*, regardless of who sees.

### 4.6 The Deep Ice

Beneath the surface, older ice contains older memories. The deepest layers contain impressions from before penguins existed. Readers who access them report alien geometries, thoughts that don't parse into penguin concepts, a sense of vast and patient attention. Some believe the deep ice *is* the ice-memory's consciousness. Others believe it is simply older recordings left by beings who were incomprehensible.

It is from these depths that artifacts of The Before Times occasionally surface — objects bearing serial numbers in no known language, radiating power that makes the ice-memory itself recoil.

## 5. The Three Peoples — Cultures

### 5.1 Aurora Stack ("The Aurora")

**Tagline.** *"The ice remembers what we choose to forget."*

**Home.** The sunlit face of Aurora Berg — a vertical colony of crystalline spires carved into volcanic highlands, warmed by vents, lit by aurora and prismatic lenses.

**Core belief: the ice remembers.** Every deed, every word, every death leaves an impression. The ice does not judge — it simply remembers. Consequences:
- Names accumulate; a penguin's name grows as the ice witnesses their deeds.
- The past is present; ancestors can be consulted through ice-reading.
- Nothing is truly lost.
- Reputation is literal.

**The Witnessed Life.** An unwitnessed deed is an incomplete deed. Hence: crowds, public announcements, carved ice-wall records. To do something alone, in secret, is to risk having it forgotten. What is forgotten might as well never have happened.

**Tier system (physically and socially).**
- **The Apex** — ice-seers, high auditors. Dwell closest to aurora; hear ice-memory most clearly. High-Beak Auditor Selka leads from here.
- **The Trade Terraces** — merchants, crafters. Most penguins. Dominated by the Icebound Exchange.
- **The Huddle Warrens** — common masses in dense communal arrangements.
- **The Foundation** — vent maintenance workers. Essential but low-status; work in darkness where ice-memory is thin.

**Prominence.** Status is measured in *prominence* — deeds witnessed, echoes earned, ice-memory's regard. Not inherited.
- **Forgotten** — no echoes, no witnesses. Essentially non-persons.
- **Marginal** — a few echoes. Most young penguins.
- **Recognized** — solid reputation.
- **Renowned** — many echoes, significant influence.
- **Mythic** — so many echoes that the ice speaks their name unbidden.

**Clans.** Marked by possessive prefixes (val', kria', rukar', molok', etc.). Clan membership is determined by **mentorship, not blood** — you belong to the clan of whoever taught you to read the ice. Clans shift constantly as prominent mentors adopt promising young penguins.

**Key practices.**
- **Ice-reading** — the most sacred practice; accessing echoes to glimpse the past and perceive the present.
- **Naming ceremonies** — when a penguin earns a new echo: deed recitation, witness confirmation, ice-reading confirmation, name extension. Often combined with feasts.
- **The Huddle** — mass warmth-sharing during Long Dark; also a spiritual practice of partial self-dissolution.
- **Counting of Names** — Long Dark recitation of the year's dead.

**The Aurora Ideal.** Live a witnessed life, accumulate many echoes, eventually become part of the ice-memory — transformed into something that will advise future generations.

### 5.2 Nightshelf ("The Shelf," "The Nightshelf")

**Tagline.** *"What is spoken can be taken. What is whispered can be traded. What is silent... is truly yours."*

**Home.** Beneath the permanent ice shelf — a labyrinth of tunnels in eternal twilight, lit by bioluminescent ice glyphs and fire-core braziers. Adapted to darkness so completely that the surface seems garish and exposed.

**Core beliefs.**

*The power of names.* Names are not labels; they are **bindings**. To know something's true name is to have power over it. Nightshelf penguins therefore never speak names completely. A penguin's true name is known only to themselves — and some say even this is partial, the deepest part known only to the shadow itself.

*Shadow as medium.* Where Aurora perceive ice-memory, Nightshelf perceive **shadow-substance** — darkness as a medium through which power flows. In sufficient darkness, the boundaries between things become negotiable. This is not metaphor; their mystics can do things in shadow that are impossible in light.

*The economy of secrets.* Information is currency. What you know determines what you are. Every conversation is a negotiation.

**The Veiled Structure.**
- **The Unspoken** — the ruling council. Members' identities unknown even to each other. Communicate through intermediaries and dead drops.
- **The Brokers** — merchants of information facilitating trades between parties who must never meet.
- **The Whisperers** — mystics who read secrets from shadow itself. Consulted for divination, employed for espionage.
- **The Veiled** — ordinary citizens in careful anonymity.
- **The Exposed** — those who have lost their secrets. Pitied and avoided.

**The Secret Markets (Void Markets).** Anything can be bought: information, services, forbidden objects, and occasionally names themselves. The Midnight Claws control most of the shadow economy.

**Shadow-Debts.** Binding agreements enforced not by law but by the shadow itself. Break too many and shadows no longer shelter you.

**Key practices.**
- **The Whisper Tongue** — communication at the edge of audibility; meaning encoded in what is *not* said. Years to learn. Maddening to outsiders.
- **Shadow-walking** — moving through darkness that seems to compress distance. Skeptics say it's excellent night-vision and silent movement. The Nightshelf do not correct them.
- **The Dreaming Pools** — sacred sites where the boundary between shadow and substance grows thin. Those who enter may gain visions, never return, or return changed. Also used as mortuary sites.
- **Incomplete rituals** — all Nightshelf ceremonies are deliberately incomplete. Weddings unfinalized, deaths not fully mourned, contracts with open clauses. Completion gives power to others; incompleteness preserves freedom.

**The Nightshelf Ideal.** A cipher — known to exist, impossible to define. Accumulate secrets without becoming known for any. Have power without anyone understanding its source. Die leaving behind unsolvable mysteries. To be *effective* without being *exposed*.

### 5.3 Orca ("The Orca," "Orca Raiders")

**Tagline.** *"We do not have names. We have histories. We do not have homes. We have hunting grounds. We do not have friends. We have... useful prey."*

**Home.** The open waters surrounding The Berg. They do not build; they claim. Territory is defined by hunting grounds and marked by the bones of their kills.

**Core beliefs.**

*You are your kills.* Orca identity is constructed entirely from deeds. A young orca's name is simple: first kill-type and method. An elder's name may be dozens of syllables — a complete record of everything they have ever killed. **An orca literally cannot introduce themselves without reciting their history.** To lie about your kills is existential theft.

*The hunt is sacred.* The fundamental activity of existence. An orca who cannot hunt is not a failed orca — they simply *are not an orca*.

*Strength deserves survival.* No mercy in the penguin sense, but a form of selection: strong prey may be allowed to escape, not from kindness but from investment. Stronger prey breeds stronger prey.

**The Pod.** 5–30 individuals, led by the eldest female (longest name). Pod loyalty is near-absolute. Orca die for their pod without hesitation. Pod membership can change — consistent non-contribution may result in being asked to leave, usually fatal since lone orca cannot hunt effectively.

**Name-hierarchy.** Status is determined by name-length. Creates a natural gerontocracy, disruptable by exceptional young hunters. Name-challenges: formal recitation contests. Dishonesty punishable by exile.

**Pod-roles (appear in names).**
- **Vor** — first-strikers, fastest, initiate attacks.
- **Grrul** — herders, drive prey to killers.
- **Thrak** — finishers, deliver killing blows.
- **Zund** — watchers, track prey and coordinate.

**Practices.**
- **Hunt-singing** — tactical vocalizations that are also psychological warfare.
- **Blood-warm gatherings** — neutral meetings at volcanic vents between pods; mating, territory negotiation, intelligence trading. Violence forbidden.
- **The Deep Descent** — when an orca can no longer hunt, they dive solo into the abyssal trenches. Do not return. This is completion, not tragedy.
- **Twice-born** — rare orca who return from failed descents. Regarded with awe and fear. Often become shamans. **Grandmother Deep** is the most revered.
- **Penguin-trading** — relationships built on mutual benefit and the constant implicit threat of reversion to prey. *"Be more useful alive than dead."*

**The Wake-Singers cult.** See Section 9.

**The Orca Ideal.** A name so long it takes minutes to recite. Lead the pod through strength and wisdom. End on your own terms in the Deep Descent. **The penguins want to be remembered; the orca want to be complete.**

## 6. Languages and Naming

### 6.1 Premise

Penguins don't experience time linearly — the ice holds memory, and names are echoes rather than labels. The three cultures think about identity differently, and their naming conventions are the visible consequence.

### 6.2 Aurora Stack — Parenthetical Echoes

Aurora names carry the ice's annotations in parentheses. These are **not optional flourishes**; they are the ice's witness to existence.

**Simple Echo** — `Name (ice-memory)`:
- Piien (twice-hatched) — survived something, reborn.
- Losek (trade-blessed) — ice saw prosperity follow them.
- Valmori (echo: Valum) — carries an ancestor's echo.
- Quillen (frost-scarred, echo: Quilara) — our annotator.

**Clan-Echo Chain** — `Name (echo) clan'Place`:
- Voraesueina (frost-written) kria'Spire
- Peiaeesi (light-descended) rukar'Reach
- Teime (ice-spoken) molok'Depths

The clan marker indicates lineage (via mentorship, not blood); the place indicates origin-territory.

**Layered Echoes** — more echoes = more history = more significance:
- Skailaen (twice-blessed, light-bearer, echo: Skai)
- Nalreniro (frost-bane, void-touched, echo: Nalren, echo: Nal)

**Sound profile.** Chinese-influenced phonetics: zh, sh, ch, x, q, j; no consonant clusters. Vowels/finals: a, o, e, i, u, ai, ei, ou, ong, ang, ing. Common roots: Xue-, Zhi-, Lian-, Feng-, Ming-, Qing-. Endings: -long, -hua, -wei, -lin, -jun. Parenthetical content uses more English sounds — the ice "translating."

### 6.3 Nightshelf — Incomplete Names

Full names hold power, so they are never spoken. Truncation marks:
- ☽ (crescent moon) — shadow has consumed part of the name.
- ∴ (therefore mark) — internal truncation, swallowed syllables.
- ~ (tilde) — voice fades to silence.

**Moon-prefix** — beginning consumed:
- ☽'varius
- ☽'ent' Vigil
- ☽'shadow Xarem~

**Moon-suffix** — ending fades:
- Umbra☽
- Night☽ Silvsomiae
- Moth☽ Shimmer

**Internal truncation** — syllables swallowed mid-word:
- Dusk∴glide
- Circle∴sea-bound~
- Apex∴enchant

**Whisper-forms** — trails into inaudibility:
- Reniava~
- Deal' Covenant~
- The Shawled Scar~

**Sound profile.** Faux-Latin: v, s, c, m, n, l, r, t; flowing, no hard stops. Vowels: a, e, i, o, u, ae, ia. Common roots: Umbr-, Noct-, Silv-, Tene-, Luc-, Sanct-. Endings: -us, -is, -ae, -ia, -um, -ius. The ☽ and ∴ are *pronounced* — brief pauses or throat-catches.

### 6.4 Orca — Deed-Names

Orca don't have names in the penguin sense. Identity IS kills, territory, pod-role.

**Kill-names** — `[Method]-[Prey]`:
- Rend-kzul (seal-render; kzul = seal)
- Gnash-threk (whale-gnasher)
- Crush-pngk (penguin-crusher)

**Accumulation** — more kills = longer name:
- Young: Kzul-rend
- Experienced: Kzul-threk-rend-vor
- Elder: Deep-kzul-threk-pngk-rend-vor-grrul (barely pronounceable)

**Sound profile (strict).** Consonants: k, g, r, th, ng, z, kh, gh, rr, d, n; hard only. Vowels: u, i, a (short), occasional schwa. Clusters *required*: kr, gr, th, ng, zk, khr, rnd, thng. **No soft sounds, no diphthongs, no articles.**

### 6.5 Differentiation at a Glance

| Feature | Aurora | Nightshelf | Orca |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parenthetical echoes `()` | core | — | — |
| Moon truncation `☽` | — | core | — |
| Internal elision `∴` | — | yes | — |
| Whisper trailing `~` | — | yes | — |
| Deed-chains | — | — | core |
| Vowel richness | high | medium | minimal |
| Consonant clusters | low | low | required |

### 6.6 Exception: Before Times Serial Numbers

Before Times artifacts resist all three naming systems. They bear alphanumeric designations like `8H-H1Y0B` or `T5-W5WJ3P`. Aurora penguins cannot add echoes — the ice refuses to annotate what it cannot comprehend. Nightshelf cannot truncate what is already incomplete. Orca cannot claim kills they did not make. Common names exist (Prime Shard, Extinction Node), but serial numbers persist in formal contexts. Documents that use the common names have a way of being lost. Penguins who speak the common names too often begin dreaming of numbers.

## 7. The Three Fires — Magic System

*"All magic costs. Aurora-light costs clarity. Captured fire costs flesh. Pressure-depth costs... something else. Something you don't notice missing until you reach for it and it's gone."*

Three separate disciplines, not schools of one. They occupy separate territories (sky, stone, deep) and do not combine. Each is bound to a culture and exacts its own price.

### 7.1 Aurora-Light (Aurora Stack)

**Domain.** Perception, preservation, pattern. Forensic magic — the magic of knowing, seeing, understanding what was and what is. **Answers questions. Does not solve problems.**

**Medium.** **Aurora-crystals** — naturally occurring formations that collect and store aurora energy like a battery. Practitioner attunes through focused meditation, channeling crystal charge into perceptive acts. Slow; cannot be cast in combat, thrown like a weapon, or used to destroy.

**Capabilities.**
- Access impressions in the ice-memory (ice-reading).
- Perceive the present state of distant locations (ice-seeing).
- At its strongest: crystallize memory into physical form — ice constructs that contain and replay specific moments.

**Cost: clarity of self.** Each use thins the boundary between practitioner's consciousness and ice-memory. Casual use costs almost nothing. Sustained use progressively erodes you: memories blur with the ice's; emotions bleed into echoes of ancient feelings not yours. Advanced readers aren't sure which memories are theirs. **High-Beak Auditor Selka's predecessor is alive but cannot tell you his own name without pausing to check which name is actually his.**

The Apex rotates readers on strict schedules — two days active, five days rest. Readers who push past this don't go mad dramatically; they become less and less distinct until they're more echo than penguin.

### 7.2 Captured Fire (Nightshelf)

**Domain.** Transformation, force, destruction. Where Aurora-light is meditation, captured fire is **detonation**.

**Medium.** **Fire-cores** — dense mineral formations pulled from The Berg's geothermal veins; compressed geothermal energy. Cracked with a specialized tool called a **venting pick**; energy released through physical contact.

**Capabilities.**
- Shape ice and stone (forge-masters).
- Heat weapons until they cut through frozen armor (warriors).
- At its strongest: unmake matter. Ice to steam. Stone to slag. A sealed door to a memory of a door.

Nightshelf forge-masters have refined core-cracking into an art — a skilled forge-master can heat a blade to cutting temperature with a core the size of a pebble, or collapse a tunnel with one the size of a fist. The Aurora use fire-cores mostly for heating, which the Nightshelf find insulting — *"like using a siege engine to crack nuts."*

**Cost: flesh.** Heat doesn't discriminate. Every use leaves burns. Minor uses leave reddened flipper-pads. Major uses leave scars that never fade. Forge-masters are recognizable by their hands: mottled, thick-skinned, patchy feathers. Veteran practitioners develop **fire-lock** — stiff joints, feathers that won't regrow, skin that splits in cold. The Nightshelf consider this an acceptable trade. They metabolize pain differently — pain is information (clean burn = clean release; ragged burn = core cracked wrong).

### 7.3 Pressure-Depth (Orca)

**Domain.** Force, communion, blood.

**Medium.** **The deep water itself.** The practitioner descends. The deeper the dive, the more power available. At sufficient depth, pressure becomes a carrier wave for intent. The practitioner opens themselves, lets the weight fill them, and surfaces carrying that charge.

**Capabilities.**
- Communicate over vast distances through subsonic vibration.
- Commune with the Dead God — or with whatever answers when they call.
- At its strongest: exert physical force without contact — the crushing weight of a mile of ocean, directed by will.

**Limited to orca by physics.** Penguins cannot dive deep enough without dying. This is not cultural — it is a hard limit. **The Dreaming Tooth amplifies the process.** Wake-Singers near the Tooth report needing shallower dives to achieve the same effects.

**Cost: "interior distance."** The closest translation from Grandmother Deep's teaching-songs. After sustained use, the practitioner becomes harder to reach — not physically, but socially, emotionally. Present but lagged. Old Wake-Singers speak in long pauses, stare at things very far down. A question may take a full minute to answer. The cost is not madness. It is distance. You become someone always partly somewhere else — somewhere deep, somewhere cold, somewhere that has a heartbeat.

### 7.4 Interference and Origins

The three fires **do not combine**. Aurora experimenters with fire-cores report the energies interfere like two sounds canceling each other. Orca who surface near aurora-crystals find their pressure-charge dissipates. Nightshelf experiments with pressure-depth relics in their forges were destructive enough that the experiments are no longer discussed.

Origins:
- Wake-Singers: the Dead God created all three.
- Aurora: the ice-memory predates them all.
- Nightshelf: the question is irrelevant — what matters is what you can reach and what you're willing to pay.

## 8. The Flipper Accord — The Mutual Hostage Arrangement

*"We signed the Accord because we were starving. They signed it because they were freezing. Nobody signed it because they wanted to."*

### 8.1 The Dependency

The Aurora Stack needs **fire-cores** from Nightshelf volcanic depths to heat their surface settlements. The Nightshelf needs **aurora-crystals** to power bioluminescent infrastructure and fuel shadow-practices. Neither can produce the other's material. Both die without it. The Accord codifies this. It does not resolve it.

### 8.2 The Terms

Carved into pressure-ice at the **Midway Shelf** (where sunlight reaches its last thin fingers before the dark). Seventeen clauses. The ones that matter:

- **Exchange Ratio** — one standard fire-core for three aurora-crystals. Renegotiated eleven times. Seven renegotiations involved casualties. Current ratio favors Nightshelf (last renegotiation happened during a desperate Long Dark).
- **Courier Guarantee** — trade caravans are inviolable. Attacking a courier is punishable by death by exposure, identically in both cultures. The body is left at Midway Shelf as a reminder. **There are seventeen skeletons there now. Some are very old.**
- **Hostage Clause** — each culture maintains a permanent delegation in the other's territory. Officially "trade ambassadors." Everyone knows they're hostages. The ambassadors know it too. **They throw good parties.**
- **Arbitration Protocol** — joint tribunal: three Aurora judges, three Nightshelf, and a seventh drawn by lot from the courier corps. In practice, the couriers decide everything, because both sides trust them more than each other. The couriers find this hilarious and terrifying.

### 8.3 History

**Before the Accord — "The Broker Wars" (Aurora) / "The Margin Disputes" (Nightshelf).** Killed more penguins than any orca raid season. Not pitched battles: knifings in dark tunnels, poisoned shipments, vanished couriers. The ice at the boundary zones is saturated with death-echoes.

**The Signing — after the Winter of Cracked Cores.** A tunnel collapse cut off the main fire-core vein; Aurora Stack lost four hundred penguins to cold exposure in six days. Nightshelf had lost their main crystal supply to a splinter dispute. Both came to the table because the alternative was extinction. On the second day the Aurora lead negotiator collapsed from hypothermia; the Nightshelf lead negotiator lent him his cloak. Aurora remember this as proof the Nightshelf have hearts. Nightshelf remember it as proof the Aurora can't dress for weather.

**Violations — 43 recorded.** Most are minor (short-weighting, delays, contraband). Worst: **the Bright Betrayal** — an Aurora faction commander seized a Nightshelf delegation and demanded triple fire-core output. The Nightshelf cut off all trade for six weeks. Two hundred Aurora froze before his own people deposed him. The Nightshelf returned the hostages unharmed with a note: *"Next time, we wait longer."* No one has seized a delegation since.

### 8.4 The Courier Corps

The Accord's circulatory system. They carry goods, deliver messages, transport hostage-ambassadors, and provide the tribunal's tiebreaking vote. The only penguins who move freely between surface and depths.

- Recruited from both cultures; belong to neither once inducted.
- Abandon cultural naming conventions; take simple trade-names: **Ferryman, Splitpath, Downdraft, Inkwell**.
- Marked with courier ink — bioluminescent fluid mixed with aurora-dust — glowing faintly in both light and dark. Impossible to disguise; both their protection and their prison.
- Expelled couriers are welcome in neither culture. Most don't last long afterward.
- **Ferrymaster Owke** runs the corps. Does not give speeches. Posts the route, posts the schedule, and trusts her couriers to come back alive. Most do.

### 8.5 The Real Accord

The written Accord is seventeen clauses on pressure-ice. The real Accord is the accumulated understanding, across generations, that **neither colony can afford to let the other die**. Not friendship. Aurora merchants don't invite Nightshelf brokers to naming ceremonies. Nightshelf brokers don't share secrets with their clients. The cultures remain alien to each other. But the shipments move, the couriers are kept safe, and the hostage-ambassadors stay comfortable enough to throw parties — because the alternative is something both cultures have tasted and do not intend to taste again.

## 9. The Dead God in the Deep

*"It is not sleeping. Sleeping implies it will wake on its own. It needs help."* — Grandmother Deep

### 9.1 The Body

Beneath **The Corpse Current**, in water so deep that light gives up, lies something two miles long, organically complex, not entirely dead. Its **heart is still beating** — one pulse every several minutes, a pulse so slow and massive you feel it in your bones before you hear it.

Orca divers confirm: the body is real, has features that resemble no living creature (not whale, not leviathan, nothing with a name), and its pulse has not sped up, slowed down, or stopped across all the ice-memory's recorded history.

### 9.2 The Corpse Current

A thermal anomaly — a river of warmer water threading through the Frozen Sea along a consistent path year after year, independent of season. During Long Dark, when the surface freezes solid, the Corpse Current keeps flowing *beneath* the ice. Orca pods follow it to survive winter.

- Smells of copper. Not strongly — enough that orca track it by scent; Aurora describe it as blood that has been sitting a very long time; Nightshelf refuse to discuss it.
- Has no geological explanation. No volcanic vents along its path. **The heat comes from the body.**
- Grandmother Deep claims the current has been growing stronger, its reach extending toward The Berg's foundations.

### 9.3 The First Contact Dive

The first confirmed contact dive was made by an orca elder now known only as **Deep-grrul-threk-kzul** — a name suggesting she was already legendary before she went down. She dove for what her pod estimated was six hours; she reported it felt like days. She surfaced with three observations:
1. The body is roughly two miles long.
2. It has features that resemble no living creature.
3. Its heart is still beating.

She died three days later. Not from the dive — from something she would not name. She simply stopped swimming and let herself sink. **The only Deep Descent anyone has ever witnessed that looked like grief.**

### 9.4 The Wake-Singers

A cult of orca shamans organized around the Dead God. Led by **Grandmother Deep**. They believe accumulated blood sacrifice at The Corpse Current will eventually rouse it.

**Two theological factions (identical rituals, different eschatologies):**
- **Orthodox** — the God will rise, consume the Frozen Sea, and remake the world into an endless hunting ground where prey never runs out and death has no meaning. A paradise of predation.
- **Reformist** (minority, tolerated) — the God will rise and destroy everything. Existence is suffering; an ending is mercy.

**Grandmother Deep** holds a third view she shares with no one in full. To the orthodox: *"It will rise."* To the reformists: *"It will end."* To neither: what she saw at the bottom.

### 9.5 The Rituals and the Dreaming Tooth

A hunt is conducted, the kill brought to The Corpse Current, the body opened, the blood drained into the water, and the Wake-Singers sing. Their songs operate at frequencies that make bones ache, harmonics no single throat should produce. Orca who listen too long report a **response from below** — not an echo, not the current, but something answering.

**The Dreaming Tooth** — a tooth the size of an orca calf, carved from what Grandmother Deep claims is the Dead God's jaw. Inscribed with kill-marks in a language that predates orca naming. Vibrates at a frequency that **makes nearby water boil in a perfect circle**. When brought to The Corpse Current during blood-offering, the current visibly accelerates.

Wielders gain power over water pressure and communion with things in the deep — but develop an increasing compulsion to feed the ocean. Three shamans have walked into the depths carrying the Tooth, never to surface. Current bearer: a young orca named **Tidescream**, who has started talking to something no one else can see.

### 9.6 Penguin Responses

- **Aurora — careful agnosticism.** Ice-readers near the coast sense "a weight" in the ice-memory, as if the body distorts memory the way a star distorts light. Official position: something is down there, probably a large dead creature; the orca's religious interpretation is their own affair.
- **Nightshelf — official silence.** Shadow-Broker Nyla has forbidden all discussion of the Dead God within Nightshelf territory. This prohibition is obeyed with the kind of obedience that suggests everyone already knows what Nyla is afraid of. Privately, shadow-walkers report that coastal darkness is **different** — denser, colder, and contains something that looks back.

### 9.7 The Heartbeat

Once you know about it, you can feel it. Stand on the coast during a still night. Press your flippers against the ice. Wait. Several minutes. A shudder. Faint. Regular. The ice records it clearly: the oldest readable layers (millennia deep) contain the same pulse, the same interval, the same weight.

Grandmother Deep says the pulse is a **countdown**. She does not say to what. When asked how much time remains, she bares her teeth and says: *"Ask me again next season."* She has said this for thirty seasons running.

## 10. The Before Times — The Apocalypse Canon

*"The ice remembers everything. Even the things that came before memory."*

Frozen in the deepest ice lie artifacts that should not exist. Impossible materials, serial numbers in no known language, power that makes the ice-memory itself recoil. The penguins call them relics of The Before Times.

### 10.1 The Six Known Artifacts

- **8H-H1Y0B — Prime Shard.** A fragment of something larger, edges impossibly sharp, surface shifting between states of matter. Held in Aurora Stack's deepest vaults. Ice-readers who approach it report hearing numbers — endless strings of them — in a voice that sounds like grinding glaciers.
- **9V-X3DV1L8 — Extinction Node.** A sphere of absolute darkness absorbing all light. Held by the Nightshelf, who claim it speaks to them in the shadow-tongue. Some believe it is not an artifact but a hole in reality.
- **T5-W5WJ3P — Ultima Core.** Recovered from the belly of a dead leviathan by orca divers. Pulses with undiminishing heat. Used by the Wake-Singers in rituals; believed to contain the Dead God's heartbeat. They may not be entirely wrong.
- **H8-5KB7Z4 — Omega Hammer.** A tool or weapon. When struck against ice, it does not break the ice — it *unmakes* it, as if the ice had never existed. The Aurora keep it sealed, terrified of misuse.
- **P3-8NL2B5H — Singularity Obelisk.** A pillar that floats precisely three inches above any surface it is placed on. Its surface is covered in the serial numbers of other artifacts — possibly a manifest of things yet to be found.
- **8R-W6GQ3K3L — Singularity Beam.** A rod emitting light of a color that has no name. Those who look upon it see different things — some see the future, some the past, some nothing at all and are never quite the same afterward.

### 10.2 Theories of Origin

- **Deep Ice Hypothesis** — remnants of a civilization that lived on The Berg millions of years ago, so ancient that even the ice-memory has compressed their impressions into incomprehensible noise.
- **Fallen Sky Theory** — debris or gifts from entities that exist in the spaces between stars; they follow no natural laws because they are foreign to this reality.
- **Future Echo Speculation** — deep-ice readers have reported seeing what feels like the future: ruined cities, lights in the sky, the ice itself burning. Perhaps the artifacts echo backward through time from something that hasn't happened yet.
- **Dead God's Tools** — Wake-Singers believe all the artifacts are pieces of the Dead God. The serial numbers are the god's true names. Assembling them all would wake it. Their greatest hope or The Berg's greatest danger, depending on whom you ask.

### 10.3 Handling Protocols

- **Aurora** — deepest vaults, surrounded by ice-readers monitoring for changes. Never touch directly. Document everything, even when documentation seems to alter itself.
- **Nightshelf** — absolute darkness. Watchers assigned from the Exposed (those with no secrets left to lose). Trade information, never the artifacts.
- **Orca** — the Wake-Singers claim custody of any artifact found in the waters. They believe the artifacts are sacred. Other pods disagree. This has led to violence.

### 10.4 The Fear

Six have been found. Serial numbers imply a series. A series implies a count. How many more exist? What happens when they are all assembled? **And most terrifying: who or what is keeping count?**

## 11. Life on The Berg

### 11.1 Food

Krill. Krill stew. Krill jerky. Krill paste on flat-ice bread. Krill fermented in brine ("salt-tang" to Aurora, "edible" to Nightshelf). Krill roe dried into sheets. Krill oil for cooking, light, waterproofing, bribing children.

Fish supplements during Endless Day: smoked, dried, or eaten raw on the docks while still twitching. **Fresh fish days are holidays in everything but name.**

The Aurora Stack's Trade Terraces host the largest food markets. Prices fluctuate with season — cheap during Endless Day, expensive approaching Long Dark, astronomical during supply disruptions.

Nightshelf cuisine is cave-fermented: mushroom-like growths on ice walls, bioluminescent algae scraped from ceilings, shadow-jar krill with flavors that don't have names in the Aurora tongue. Most Aurora don't bother acquiring the taste.

### 11.2 Work

- **Ice-cutters** — harvest clear ice from upper glaciers with fire-core-heated picks. Employs more penguins than any other trade.
- **Krill-runners** — fishing boats and shore-nets in open water. Dangerous (leopard seals). Seasonal. A good krill-runner earns in four months what they need for twelve.
- **Fire-core miners** — work the Nightshelf's volcanic veins. Cave-ins, gas pockets, veins that crack wrong and release energy sideways. Same scarred flippers as forge-masters, less status and less pay.
- **Couriers** — walk the Accord route.
- **Builders** — Aurora in ice and stone on the surface; Nightshelf in compressed ice and volcanic glass underground. Both use fire-cores for joining and shaping. Both lose people to workplace accidents with scandalous regularity.

### 11.3 Children

**Warming-chambers** — fire-core heated rooms where dozens of eggs are tended by rotating caregivers. Deep enough to maintain temperature even in Long Dark. **The most heavily guarded spaces on The Berg.** An orca raiding party that threatened a warming-chamber would unite both cultures faster than any diplomat ever managed.

Young penguins spend their first years in the Huddle Warrens, learning from whatever adults are available — informal, collective education. Aurora formalize this into clan-mentorship around age three. Nightshelf formalize nothing, which is itself a formalization: a young Nightshelf penguin is expected to figure out who knows what and negotiate for the knowledge. This is the first lesson in the Nightshelf's true curriculum.

### 11.4 Entertainment

- **Story-recitation** — the dominant art form. Aurora recite competitively in the Trade Terraces. Accuracy is for ice-readers; story-recitation is for feeling something.
- **Music** — percussion dominates: ice-drums, stone-taps, rhythmic body-slapping. Nightshelf throat-singing at frequencies the Aurora find physically uncomfortable.
- **Gambling** — universal. Catch sizes, block weights, where the leopard seal will show up. Nightshelf run a shadow-economy of **information-bets** — wagers where the winner gets knowledge and the loser owes a truth.
- **Sliding** — the closest thing to sport. Belly-slide competitions. Aurora hold formal matches in Endless Day. Nightshelf hold informal ones in the dark.

### 11.5 The Small Hours

Most of life on The Berg is not politics, magic, or orca raids. It is a penguin at a cook-pot, stirring krill stew. Two couriers sharing a flask at Midway Shelf, complaining about the cold because complaining is a social activity. A chick falling asleep against a warming-chamber wall while an old miner tells a hundred-times-told story about a fire-core vein so pure it sang when you cracked it. A shore-watcher coming off rotation, nodding to penguins she knows by face but not by name. **The extraordinary happens; when it does, everyone is very tired afterward, and very hungry, and someone always asks what's for dinner.**

## 12. Death on The Berg

*"We don't bury our dead. Where would we dig?"*

### 12.1 Causes

1. **Cold exposure** — the leading cause. Not dramatic freezing — the slow, insidious kind. The cold doesn't hunt. It just waits.
2. **Orca predation** — second. Violent and witnessed, so disproportionate weight in cultural memory.
3. **Workplace accidents** — third. Mining cave-ins, ice-cutting mishaps, fire-core misfires, construction falls.
4. **Malnutrition** — disease is rare (cold kills most pathogens); hunger is not. Penguins who starve die quietly in the Huddle, surrounded by others eating slightly less to give slightly more.
5. **The Walking** — a few each Long Dark. Nobody counts these as suicide, because nobody is sure that's what they are.

### 12.2 Aurora Mortuary Practice — Return to the Ice

The body is carried to the nearest exposed ice face (glacier wall, crevasse edge, pressure ridge) and placed against it. An ice-reader performs the **Witnessing** — a formal reading of the death-impression. Two purposes: confirms manner of death for record, and ensures the ice-memory has properly received the deceased.

A penguin whose death the ice did not record is considered **truly dead** — gone without echo. This is the worst fate an Aurora penguin can imagine. It almost never happens, but the fear drives the entire practice.

After the Witnessing, the body is left. The cold preserves; the ice absorbs. Over centuries, the dead become a geological layer. **The Aurora do not have graveyards. They have The Berg itself. Every surface they walk on contains their ancestors.**

**The Counting of Names** — Long Dark recitation of every penguin who died since the last one. Full names with all echoes. Crowd responds *"Witnessed."* Bad years run three days straight in shifts. Nobody leaves. The exhaustion is the point — you feel the weight of the dead in your body, not just your mind. A penguin whose name is not included has been forgotten; in Aurora culture, worse than death itself.

### 12.3 Nightshelf Mortuary Practice — Completion

In life, every Nightshelf penguin keeps their name deliberately incomplete. **In death, the full name is spoken aloud for the first and only time** — by whoever held the most shadow-debts with the departed. The speaking happens in absolute darkness. No bioluminescence, no fire-cores, no glow-fissure light. The name is spoken into the dark, and the dark takes it. **In life, an incomplete name was protection. In death, completion is freedom.**

The body is then lowered into the **Dreaming Pools**. Organic matter dissolves over weeks. The dead join the shadow-substance that fills the Nightshelf's deepest chambers. Every shadow-walk, every whisper-reading, every act of Nightshelf magic is performed through a medium that **contains the dead**. They do not find this disturbing — they find it obvious.

**Incomplete mourning.** No formal period. No public ceremonies beyond the name-speaking. Completed grief would give the dead power over the living. So grief is carried, never fully resolved. A Nightshelf penguin may hold a dozen unfinished griefs at once. Outsiders see Nightshelf emotional reserve and call it coldness. It is the opposite: **a society so saturated with unfinished mourning that composure is the only alternative to collapse.**

### 12.4 Orca Mortuary Practice — The Deep Descent

No mortuary practice. When an orca can no longer hunt, they dive solo into the abyssal trenches. Do not return. This is **completion**, not tragedy. The pod recites the departed's full name — every kill, every deed — and then stops speaking it. A finished name is meaningless to speak.

Orca who die before they can descend — killed in combat, injury, accident — are **incomplete**. Their names are never recited in full; the final kill-segment is left unspoken. Pods carry these incomplete names as cautionary data: *this is how that orca died. This is what was left undone. Learn from it or join it.*

**Grandmother Deep is the exception.** Twice-born: descended and returned. Her name should be finished, but she kept hunting. She kept accumulating kills. Her name grows past the point where it should have stopped. Orca theories: she cheated death; death let her go; she hasn't actually returned yet — the orca swimming among them is something the deep sent back wearing her name. She does not clarify. She just keeps swimming.

## 13. The Five Eras of Recorded History

Chronicled (with characteristic skepticism) by Quillen (frost-scarred, echo: Quilara) val'Foundation, Third Under-Archivist of the Foundation Depths.

### 13.1 The Great Thaw

Age of expansion and optimism. The great ice sheets retreat, revealing new waters rich with krill and ancient secrets frozen beneath. Bold explorers from both colonies claim territory and found settlements. Guild halls rise, their charters written on pressed ice. Orca pods patrol distant deep currents — stories traded in hushed tones at the ice-edge but no direct contact. Every marginal rookery dreams of becoming a great berg.

**Quillen's view.** *"An age of expansion and optimism that lasted precisely as long as the krill held out, which was not as long as the songs suggest. Colonies were founded. Guilds were chartered. Everyone was very excited about the future. The ice from this period has a particular quality — a brightness, almost, as if even the frost believed things were going well. The most difficult era to read clearly, not because impressions are faint but because they are so thoroughly contaminated by enthusiasm."*

### 13.2 The Faction Wars

Trade routes become battle lines. Old alliances fracture along cultural fault lines. Factions emerge within colonies; each claims the true path to survival. Heroes rise from war. Orca raiding parties grow bolder along outer currents — isolated fishing crews vanish; border colonies report strange songs from the deep.

**Quillen's view.** *"The inevitable consequence of too many colonies and not enough krill, dressed up in ideology to make it seem like it was about something other than food. I am aware this is an unpopular interpretation. I am also aware that the casualty figures in the standard accounts do not add up, and that several of the most celebrated 'battles' were closer to what an honest historian would call riots. The ice is not kind to this era. It remembers the dead with more clarity than the living."*

### 13.3 The Clever Ice Age

War-weary civilizations turn to cleverness rather than conflict. Guilds establish schools of magic and workshops of invention, competing to unlock fire-core crystals and spectralum lenses. Aurora mystics codify old ice-memory rituals while Nightshelf engineers devise large-scale krill-harvesting machines. Age of wonders. From the outer waters, darker whispers: orca war-pods massing, scouts circling closer, testing defenses that have yet to be built.

**Quillen's view.** *"An era that produced genuine wonders and genuine horrors in roughly equal measure, though the official histories have sorted these into separate piles and labeled one 'progress' and the other 'unfortunate incidents.' The workshops of this age gave us fire-core innovations and spectralum optics. They also gave us things that the ice has recorded but that I will not describe here, because some of what I have read in the deep layers beneath the old guild halls should not be in a foreword. Or anywhere."*

### 13.4 The Orca Incursion

The orca come not as raiders but as **conquerors**, their war-pods striking with coordination that speaks of dark intelligence. Old feuds between Aurora and Nightshelf fade as colonies band together against extinction. Innovations of the previous age become weapons. The heroes of this era are defenders holding the line at impossible odds. Each culture contributes what it knows best: Shelf shadow-craft and espionage, Stack mystical barriers, and the desperate courage of those with nowhere left to retreat.

**Quillen's view.** *"The era most consistently misrepresented in popular accounts. I say this not to diminish the courage of those who fought — it was real, and the ice remembers it vividly — but to note that the orcas did not arrive without reason, and that the reasons are less flattering to penguin-kind than we prefer to acknowledge. I have read ice-testimony from the Hunting Currents that complicates the standard narrative considerably."*

### 13.5 The Frozen Peace

The orca threat recedes, leaving generational scars. Survivors rebuild colonies and the bonds between cultures, forging new traditions that blend Aurora mysticism with Nightshelf shadow-craft. Great festivals return, tinged with remembrance for the fallen. Guilds that once competed cooperate. Every new chick represents a victory against oblivion.

**Quillen's view.** *"The Frozen Peace is not peace. It is exhaustion dressed as stability, and I say this with genuine affection for the exhausted."*

### 13.6 The Transition (present)

The Council has commissioned an annotated chronicle "in preparation for what they are calling the transition." No one will say what the transition is. Quillen has read enough history to recognize the pattern: institutions commission annotated editions of their chronicles when they sense an ending — or a beginning so large the previous era must be formally closed and shelved. The Great Thaw warranted one when the Faction Wars began. The Faction Wars warranted one when the fighting stopped. The Clever Ice Age warranted one when the orcas arrived and rendered cleverness insufficient.

**Something is coming.** Quillen writes: *"The ice knows. I can feel it in the deep layers — a change in the quality of the impressions, a shift in what the frost chooses to preserve. The ice is recording more carefully now, the way it does before events it considers significant. I do not know what it is preparing to witness."*

Dated: *"The fifty-third year of the Frozen Peace — or, if we are being precise about it, the third year after the Frozen Peace ended and no one bothered to name what came next."*

## 14. Named People (Seed NPCs)

### 14.1 High-Beak Auditor Selka — Aurora Stack

Meticulous mayor of Aurora Stack. Elected by a demonstration of aurora-magic so precise she etched trade figures into ice from across a room. Speaks in numbers and celestial metaphors. Silver-threaded robes, always immaculate. **Crystal circlet calibrated to detect lies through subtle temperature shifts.** Rose through the Icebound Exchange before taking office; critics note she has never ruled against the guild's interests. Her office contains **seventeen aurora-lenses** trained on different parts of the colony, and she is said to watch them all simultaneously. The Nightshelf calls her *"the Counting Beak"* and trusts her exactly as far as the Flipper Accord requires.

### 14.2 Shadow-Broker Nyla — Nightfall Shelf

Unofficial mayor of Nightfall Shelf. Holds no elected office; her word carries more weight than any council vote. Inherited the Midnight Claws at nineteen after her predecessor fell into a fire-vent — an accident she was suspiciously positioned to survive. Ash-gray wraps that blend into tunnel shadows. Speaks in riddles that reveal themselves three conversations later. Her left flipper bears burn scars from a forge accident she won't discuss. **Never been seen in aurora-light.** Her power peaks in Long Dark. She has forbidden all discussion of the Dead God within Nightshelf territory.

### 14.3 Keeper Hollows — The Drowned Ledge

Last miner to escape the Drowned Ledge, now a hermit who camps at the sealed entrance and refuses to leave. Young when the flooding came; old enough now that the memories haven't faded as they should. Claims to hear **knocking from below the ice-locks** — rhythmic patterns matching no mining code. Feathers gone gray-white from decades without proper light. Trades information about the old tunnels for food, fire-cores, and promises no one will unseal the doors. The Midnight Claws have tried to relocate him three times; he keeps returning. Owns **The Hollows Chart**, a water-damaged mining survey map whose deeper sections are annotated in a hand he swears isn't his — though it's clearly his own.

### 14.4 Grandmother Deep — Orca, leader of the Wake-Singers

Oldest orca matriarch in living memory. Dove to touch the Dead God's body and returned changed. Her left eye sees the present; her right eye, clouded white, sees something else — futures, pasts, or simply bone, depending on who's asking. Leads no pod directly; advises all of them. Whale-song carries prophecies that have proven accurate often enough to terrify. Remembers when penguins were just food, before they built towers and channeled lights. Curious why they've changed, and whether the change makes them more or less suitable for sacrifice. The scars across her flank spell words in a language the Dead God's followers haven't used in generations. The twice-born whose name keeps growing past where it should have stopped.

### 14.5 Ferrymaster Owke — Courier Corps (implied)

Runs the Courier Corps of the Flipper Accord with the grim efficiency of someone who has buried more colleagues than she can count. Does not give speeches. Posts the route, posts the schedule, and trusts her couriers to come back alive. Established the two-weeks-on-four-weeks-off shore-watch rotation after losing three watchers in a single Long Dark to the Walking.

### 14.6 Quillen (frost-scarred, echo: Quilara) val'Foundation — Aurora Stack

Third Under-Archivist of the Foundation Depths. Thirty-one years reading the deep ice. Author of the commissioned *Annotated Chronicle* covering the five eras. Considered by her own admission "opinionated, occasionally melancholy, irritable about sloppy sourcing, quietly furious about empirically incoherent casualty figures in standard accounts." Has outlasted her own institutional justification (the committee that appointed her has been dissolved). Prefers not to investigate whether this is evidence of her importance or an administrative oversight. Her two assistants were reassigned eighteen months before her commission to "forward-facing initiatives." She works alone.

### 14.7 Tidescream — Orca Wake-Singer

Young orca, current bearer of **The Dreaming Tooth**. Has started talking to something no one else can see. Three prior bearers walked into the depths carrying the Tooth and never surfaced.

### 14.8 Deep-grrul-threk-kzul — historical orca

The legendary elder who made the first confirmed contact dive to the Dead God's body. Six-hour dive (felt like days). Surfaced with three observations. Died three days later — not from the dive but from something she would not name. Let herself sink in the only Deep Descent anyone has ever witnessed that looked like grief.

## 15. Factions

### 15.1 The Icebound Exchange — Aurora Stack

The merchant guild that controls Aurora Stack's trade routes. Ledgers track every aurora-crystal harvested and every fire-core imported from the Nightshelf below. **Founded after the Faction Wars** when three competing trading families merged rather than destroy each other. Operates from a crystalline hall where deals are sealed under aurora-light and broken contracts are recorded in ice that never melts. Symbol: interlocked flippers holding a prism — marks warehouses, ships, and the foreheads of debtors who couldn't pay. High-Beak Auditor Selka chairs the council, though some whisper she serves the Exchange more than it serves the colony.

### 15.2 The Midnight Claws — Nightfall Shelf

The syndicate that rules Nightfall Shelf's shadow economy — smuggling, enforcement, and the quiet disappearances the Fire Keepers pretend don't happen. Emerged from the tunnel gangs of the Faction Wars. Shadow-Broker Nyla leads. Maintains control through informants, blackmail ledgers, and the simple fact that crossing the Claws means your body feeds the deep vents. Mark their territory with claw-scratches that glow faintly in darkness — bioluminescent ink mixed with something organic.

### 15.3 The Wake-Singers — Orca

Cult of orca shamans who believe accumulated blood sacrifice will wake the Dead God. Grown bolder in recent years — raids target not just fishing parties but settlements, stockpiling captives in underwater caves for rituals that leave the water red for days. Wear bone headdresses carved from their own ancestors. Sing at frequencies that make ice crack and penguins' hearts stutter. Grandmother Deep guides theology; younger shamans handle operational details (kidnapping, containment, systematic drowning). **They believe they're close to a breakthrough. They believe the god has started dreaming.**

### 15.4 The Fire Keepers — Nightfall Shelf (referenced)

Control the volcanic cores that warm the Nightshelf tunnels. Tolerate the Midnight Claws because the alternative is chaos. The Claws tolerate them because someone has to keep the cores burning.

## 16. Places (Seed Locations)

### 16.1 Aurora Berg

The supernatural iceberg itself. Large enough to contain mountains, caverns, and civilizations that have never seen its edges. Drifts under perpetual twilight. Something vast moves in the darkness beneath the frozen sea. The aurora pulses with power even the oldest mystics do not fully understand.

### 16.2 Aurora Stack

Vertical colony on the sunlit face. Crystalline spires, aurora-channeling prismatic lenses, white robes, astronomical metaphors. Mystics glow faintly from within; their magic manifests as crystalline growth and temperature drops. Dependent on Nightshelf fire-cores.

### 16.3 Nightfall Shelf

Labyrinthine colony in the shadowed interior. Bioluminescent ice glyphs, fire-core braziers, coded proverbs. Dependent on Aurora aurora-crystals.

### 16.4 The Glow-Fissure

A deep crack beneath Nightfall Shelf pulsing with cold blue-green light no fire-core produces, rhythmic as breathing. Appeared three generations ago when miners broke through into a void that shouldn't exist at that depth. Descenders report whale-song and skull-aching pressure changes. Midnight Claws post guards "to prevent accidents." Unofficially, **Shadow-Broker Nyla has forbidden anyone from mapping what lies below, and the last penguin who tried came back speaking a language no one recognized.**

### 16.5 The Drowned Ledge

Abandoned Nightshelf mining outpost, silent for three generations after its deepest tunnel breached something that shouldn't have held water. Flooding came fast — seawater cold enough to kill in minutes, pouring through ice-locks that had held for decades. Survivors sealed upper tunnels and fled, leaving equipment, bodies, and whatever the miners found before the breach. Fire-core braziers still glow faintly through frozen water, visible through ice windows that no one approaches after dark. Official records (kept by the Midnight Claws, not famous for honesty) list it as a geological accident.

### 16.6 The Corpse Current

Underwater thermal convergence where currents meet and temperatures run warmer than physics should allow. Fish swim in spirals, caught in patterns they can't escape. Debris from the deep surfaces coated in frost that won't melt. Orca call it the place where the Dead God's blood still circulates; their shamans pilgrimage to float in waters that taste of iron and age. **No penguin has ever seen it** — a closely guarded secret shared only among Wake-Singers. Grandmother Deep claims the current is growing stronger, its reach extending toward The Berg's foundations.

### 16.7 The Still Pools

Geothermally-heated lagoon in a crater at The Berg's far edge, perpetually shrouded in steam. Settled generations ago by Aurora Stack dissidents who rejected the Flipper Accord and its endless politics for deliberate isolation. Fish the warm waters, tend small ice-gardens, maintain strict no-contact with either colony. No census ever counted them; no map officially marks their location. **The steam is thinning as the vents fail** — the oldest residents know their hidden paradise has perhaps a decade left before the mists clear and the outside world comes looking.

### 16.8 The Veil Vents

Geothermal vents ringing the Still Pools that produce the concealing steam. Tap into the same volcanic system that powers Nightshelf fire-cores, though the Still Pools residents have never made the connection. **Output declining for decades.** The oldest vent went cold last winter. Residents tend remaining vents like sacred flames, feeding them fish bones and aurora-crystals, praying to forces they don't understand for a miracle geology cannot provide.

### 16.9 The Forgotten Shore

Mist-choked berg-edge where the Frozen Sea meets the Still Pools crater. Explorers see only fog and assume there's nothing beyond; the few who push through emerge confused, memories of the passage fuzzy and unreliable. Residents maintain subtle markers to guide themselves home. One Aurora cartographer reached it a decade ago and noted *"persistent fog, no features of interest"* before turning back. Residents found his abandoned survey flag and still use it to mark where the mists grow thick enough to hide them.

### 16.10 Midway Shelf

Neutral transit point where sunlight reaches its last thin fingers before the dark begins. Site of the Accord's pressure-ice slab. Courier checkpoint. **Seventeen skeletons** of Accord-violators lie here as permanent reminders.

## 17. Artifacts (Seed)

### 17.1 The Hollows Chart

A mining survey map recovered from the Drowned Ledge. Water-damaged. Above a certain depth, the notation is standard Nightshelf. Below it, the markings change: geometric symbols, depth measurements in units no guild uses, and a large chamber labeled only with a glyph that makes readers' eyes water. Keeper Hollows found it in his pack after the flooding, though he has no memory of drawing it. The handwriting is clearly his own. The Midnight Claws have offered substantial payment; he has refused every time. He sleeps with it pressed against his chest.

### 17.2 The Dreaming Tooth

A tooth larger than an adult orca, carved from the Dead God's jaw by a shaman whose name has been deliberately forgotten. Hums at frequencies that resonate with pressure-magic. Those who hold it too long report dreams of drowning in warm darkness while something vast watches with patient hunger. Wake-Singers keep it at the Corpse Current's heart, using it to focus rituals and amplify songs. Wielders gain power over water pressure and can speak to things in the deep — but develop an increasing compulsion to feed the ocean. Three shamans have walked into the depths carrying it, never to surface. Current bearer: Tidescream.

## 18. Pressures (World-Tension Axes, for Simulation Context)

The world has four measured pressures that rise and fall with events:

- **Resource Availability** — unused-resource buffer. Positive = plenty; negative = crisis. Grows with resource nodes; shrinks with colonies.
- **Magical Stability** — how stable the Berg's native magic is. Positive = less wild magic; negative = frequent anomalies. Cults stabilize wild magic for their purposes; criminal syndicates destabilize through artifact smuggling and black-market magic trade.
- **Iceberg Security** — defense against external threat (primarily orca). Positive = secure; negative = looming threat.
- **Cultural Harmony** — inter-culture peace. Positive = little strife; negative = war could break out at any point.

Era transitions trigger pressure swings. The Great Thaw begins with magical stability rising and ends with resource availability and cultural harmony dropping. The Faction Wars crush cultural harmony. The Orca Incursion slams iceberg security to −40.

## 19. Framing Notes for Narrative Use

- **Central metaphysical conceit.** The ice remembers. This is a recording universe, not a just one. Nothing is erased, but nothing is punished. The dead persist not as spirits with intent but as impressions with personality.
- **Central political conceit.** The Flipper Accord. Two civilizations kept alive by a trade dependency neither wanted. Not peace. Not friendship. A mutual hostage arrangement dressed in trade language.
- **Central cosmological conceit.** The Frozen Sea has no edge, and something two miles long lies at the bottom of it with its heart still beating.
- **Central temporal conceit.** The transition. The Frozen Peace ended three years ago and nobody bothered to name what came next. The Council commissions chronicles when an era is about to close. Quillen can feel the ice recording more carefully.
- **Three irreducible ways of being a person.** Aurora: accumulate echoes, be witnessed, join the ice. Nightshelf: hold secrets, remain incomplete, join the shadow. Orca: accumulate kills, be complete, descend on your own terms.
- **Three irreducible costs for power.** Aurora-light costs clarity of self. Captured fire costs flesh. Pressure-depth costs interior distance.
- **The great unresolved questions.**
  - What is the transition?
  - What does the Dead God's heartbeat count down to?
  - How many Before Times artifacts exist, and what keeps count?
  - What lies below the Glow-Fissure?
  - What is beyond the Frozen Sea?
  - Who annotated the deep sections of the Hollows Chart?
  - What returned as Grandmother Deep?

These are not puzzles the setting intends to solve. They are the pressure under the ice.
