# The Faction Wars

> **Thesis.** The preceding volume argued that the Great Thaw manufactured every culture's defining vulnerability under the sign of progress — administrative precision for the Aurora Stack, institutional secrecy for the Nightshelf, theological patience for the orca pods — while the ice documented trajectories no one could read. The Faction Wars is what those instruments do when activated by scarcity: they function. The Aurora Stack's consensus protocols distributed suffering through the same procedures that had distributed abundance, and no specification in the system distinguished between the two operations. The Nightshelf's secrecy infrastructure consumed its own practitioners with the same rigor it had used to protect them, its foundational doctrine literally rewritten from 'what is shared is power multiplied' to 'what is hoarded is power preserved' between one ice stratum and the next. The Flipper Accord allocated deprivation with the same formality it had allocated fire-cores, renegotiated in secret three times before anyone acknowledged what it had become. The orca pods' theological patience bore fruit not as conquest but as permanent descent — the one faction that achieved its institutional objective did so by leaving penguin politics entirely. And beneath all institutional activity, something sealed responded to the era's disturbances as geology responds to pressure: without opinion, without alignment, without any mechanism for caring which faction held which corridor. The world did not break during the Faction Wars. It performed. The horror survivors carried into the Clever Ice Age was not the memory of systems failing but the discovery that the systems had worked exactly as designed, under conditions the designers had mistaken for impossible, and no one could locate the mechanism by which working machinery might be made to stop producing what it was producing. The Faction Wars did not destroy the Thaw's institutions. It revealed what they had always been.

---

The krill-blooms thinned. The colonies counted their stores, and the stores answered with arithmetic the councils could not outvote, and the counting — meticulous, honest, performed by competent administrators under clear aurora — set the terms for everything that followed. No system failed during the Faction Wars. Every protocol functioned. The Aurora Stack convened its councils and held its votes beneath skies that did not cloud often enough to postpone what needed postponing. The Nightshelf sealed its tunnels and kept its secrets and amended its doctrine with heat-work applied directly to the foundational ice. The Flipper Accord allocated fire-cores by formula and distributed the allocations by cart and courier and armed escort. And the ice beneath — the deep strata that the Thaw's expansion had drilled into, mined through, built upon with the confidence of an age that mistook geology for gift — recorded the whole of it with the patience of a medium that does not distinguish between the civilization that disturbed it and the pressure that sealed it.

Thirty-five years. Two factions that needed each other and could not forgive the needing. A third power — patient, liturgical, toothed — that waited for the defenses to fragment and pursued an objective entirely outside the framework of the war. And beneath all three, something sealed before any penguin set flipper to ice began, for the first time in the record, to count.

---

The Smelt burned in the thirty-third year after the Melt.

The Nightshelf's only forge-grade facility — the furnace complex sunk into the volcanic seam where raw fire-cores were processed into usable warmth, light, and trade goods — collapsed in a structural fire that three independent surveys attributed to three incompatible causes. The ice preserves all three reports. None accounts for the stress patterns in the surrounding strata, which indicate pressure from below inconsistent with any surface ignition. What the ice preserves beyond dispute is what followed.

The Nightshelf retained its volcanic tunnels, its raw cores, its extraction crews working the deep seams by lamp and memory. It lost every means of converting what they pulled from the earth into what their people needed to survive. The faction that had controlled the entire fire-core supply chain — mine to furnace to finished product — woke to find itself dependent on Aurora Stack processing capacity for the single most critical resource on the berg.

The Aurora Stack did not seize this advantage. The institutional machinery seized it for them. The Flipper Accord's allocation formulas had been written during the Thaw, when the Nightshelf processed its own cores and the formulas governed only the surplus traded upward. Now the same formulas governed survival itself. The language had not changed. The arithmetic had not changed. What had changed was the weight the arithmetic carried — and no clause in the Accord distinguished between allocating abundance and rationing despair.

Three renegotiations were conducted in secret before the public version was signed. The drafts survive frozen in administrative ice beneath the eastern galleries, complete with margin annotations in at least four hands, two unattributable to any known negotiator. The first predates the incident at Terrace Momiou by two full rotations. Whatever the formal histories record as the Accord's breaking point came long after the Accord had already been broken, mended in private, and broken again in the same private, by the same hands.

Seven renegotiations across the era involved casualties. The reports round conspicuously.

Fire-core shipments became the war's instrument. The Nightshelf restricted raw core access to Aurora processing facilities, creating bottlenecks that starved the very furnaces meant to process Nightshelf material. The Aurora Stack responded with delays calibrated to the day — three days' short-weighting forced a colony to choose between heating its nurseries and lighting its council chambers, and the choice was always documented, and the documentation was always produced at the next renegotiation as evidence of need or evidence of waste, depending on who held the report. Each faction gripped a blade by the edge and called the bleeding leverage.

The councils voted on every restriction. At the ruins of the Smelt settlement — repurposed as a negotiation site with the irony only institutions can produce without intending — the vote ran eight to three to fortify rather than accept the proposal to surrender forge-grade ore reserves now against collapse later. This was the pattern across forty pre-war councils preserved in the ice. The option that demanded grief before it delivered salvation lost to the option that permitted action without reckoning, and the vote was always close enough that both sides could claim the consensus had spoken, and the consensus had always spoken, and the consensus had always chosen the slower death.

---

The dependency cracked the berg along a line no one had drawn on any map.

Geography had organized the Thaw. Aurora penguins lived in the upper stacks and along the eastern shelves; Nightshelf clans held the tunnels and the western volcanic reaches; trading posts occupied the margins between. The Flipper Accord governed these boundaries with the serene confidence of a document that assumed its signatories would remain where they were. They did not.

Fire-core scarcity forced penguins into the tunnels to trade, to beg, to steal. Tunnel culture — its codes, its ellipses, its layered silence — began to separate those who could navigate it from those who could not, and the separation cut across every prior allegiance. An Aurora penguin who learned to read Nightshelf proverbs and trade in implications had more in common with the tunnel-runners than with the Council of Luminaries convening under clear sky. A Nightshelf miner who believed hoarding knowledge served no one found the Aurora Stack's open councils revelatory — and discovered that openness, as Aurora practiced it, was its own performance, its own consensus theater, and no more honest than silence. Ideological alignment replaced geography. Penguins who believed in transparency and those who believed in concealment could not share corridors regardless of where they had been hatched. The Flipper Accord's governance framework, built to manage a geographic boundary, discovered that the boundary had moved inside every colony, every family, every tunnel where two penguins had once passed each other with a nod and now pressed against opposite walls.

The Pinnacle faction, crystallized during the worst of the sorting, compressed its theology into a recognition formula spoken between members: *We chose, we stayed, we're still here.* Survival reframed as election. Departure reframed as divine judgment — those who remained were chosen; those who left were found wanting. The formula worked. It always worked. Across every era the ice has preserved, some version of that claim appears in the mouth of whatever faction needed to believe its suffering was selection. The cost compounds separately and is never included in the account.

The Nightshelf's Shadow Parliament — meeting in rotating locations, its membership unconfirmed, its proceedings unrecorded in any medium the Aurora Stack could subpoena — was structurally designed for exactly this kind of recruitment. It absorbed ideological converts the way it absorbed everything: silently, completely, without acknowledgment. The Aurora Stack's consensus system, which required public demonstration of aurora-magic for candidacy, could not absorb anyone who preferred shadow. It expelled its own dissenters with procedural courtesy and watched them walk into tunnels it did not control.

Into the vacuum, the Midnight Claws.

They rose from the tunnel gangs that had moved goods through passages too narrow or too dangerous for sanctioned couriers. Shadow-Broker Nyla consolidated control through blackmail and murder — and Nyla's predecessor had been an Aurora penguin, not Nightshelf, though the official histories were quietly amended to obscure this. The original tunnel networks the Claws inherited had been built by someone fleeing one faction and unwelcome in the other. The consolidation succeeded because ideological sorting had already dissolved the community bonds that might have resisted criminal governance. The Claws offered alignment to neither doctrine: loyalty to the tunnel itself, to whoever controlled the route the fire-core shipment traveled, to the practical theology of warmth delivered on time. They collected debts. They left rations for those who would not leave their posts. They killed those who threatened the supply chain and mourned no one publicly. The formal Accord continued operating — votes held, allocations debated, consensus performed beneath aurora-light — while actual governance moved through passages that answered to no charter.

The Nightshelf sharpened its primary cross-factional weapon. The Foedus silenti — the silence law — had begun as internal discipline, a codification of the conviction that knowledge shared was knowledge vulnerable. Under wartime pressure the silence law became an instrument of asymmetric control at every negotiating table. It criminalized the disclosure of casualty figures past certain thresholds, tunnel routes, the nature of what the deep ice was doing in the western galleries. Aurora representatives at Accord renegotiations found themselves unable to reference what the silence law forbade naming, even when both parties at the table knew the facts. Every diplomatic exchange became a code exercise in which one side held the cipher.

The cost compounded inward. A Nightshelf chronicler — Houchaiqangwei, whose hand is identifiable across eleven surviving documents — kept writing as aurora-ink was rationed, her script shrinking with each dispatch, the letters tightening until the final records require magnification to read. She could not report supply-line collapse in direct language. She could not name casualties in plain figures. She wrote what the silence law permitted, in a hand that grew smaller and smaller, and the shrinking was not metaphor. It was a woman running out of material who understood that not-writing was the only thing worse than writing in a script no one could see.

In the memorial corridors, a Remembrancer named Obscureth~ carved what was mandated and made the gaps between inscriptions legible — the space where a name should have been and was not, the line break that fell where no line break was earned, the chisel-mark that stopped a fraction too soon. Other Remembrancers read the stone itself, and learned from the gaps what the silence law forbade the inscriptions from saying. Her knees wore grooves in the corridor floor. Her lamp had three months of fuel and no requisition pending. The whetstone wedged between memorial stones was the only one in that passage, because the work had narrowed to a single pair of flippers in a corridor where the budget had gone dark before the light did.

---

The Aurora Stack governed the crisis with the full weight of its apparatus, and the apparatus did not falter, and the crisis deepened with each session convened and quorum met.

Audits were filed in triplicate with the crystalline precision the Thaw had institutionalized. Competent subordinates attended every session, recorded every motion, cross-referenced every allocation against the master ledger. An architect named Xouhoulong designed structures of extraordinary functional elegance for the settlements that needed them — plazas that funneled aurora-light for maximum illumination, antechambers built for diplomatic confidentiality, research galleries sealed against contamination. Every structure performed its designed function and conducted what its designer had not anticipated. The plaza that funneled light also funneled the Wake-Singer hymns drifting up from the lower passages. The antechamber incubated the secrets that corroded the next renegotiation. The sealed galleries became corridors for the corruption they had been sealed to exclude. Ice does not punish. It conducts. What you channel through it arrives where the geometry directs, and the geometry serves no faction and honors no intention.

A subordinate named Lianoshing carried the schematics between sessions, carried the supply-chain reports, carried the knowledge of what was failing and where. Present for every critical assessment. Permitted to act in none. The ice record catalogues at least eleven such figures across the era — competent, informed, positioned at the exact juncture where intervention would have mattered, held in place by an architecture that granted proximity to decision without authority to decide. The same consensus protocols that prevented any single actor from seizing power prevented any single actor from exercising judgment, and the distinction between those two operations was invisible to the system that performed both.

Selka died in a Calving at the eastern galleries while cataloguing discrepancies she had been accumulating for the better part of a season. The official account attributes the fracture to natural compression. The deep ice shows stress patterns propagating from inside, originating at a depth consistent with tunnels opened three weeks prior under an orca diplomatic pact that neither faction's public record acknowledges. No proof is required beyond what the ice already shows: the eastern face fractured from within, someone had opened tunnels beneath it, and the penguin cataloguing structural anomalies died in the event.

A replacement arrived. Qeihong carried the confidence of distance — the assessor who had never chosen between doctrine and a colony going dark. His distinction was sharp: *Protecting the colony is not the same as protecting your vision of the colony.* Within two cycles the ice records comparable compromises under his authority. The line survives in citation because it flatters the reader who believes they would have chosen differently. They would not have. The apparatus that produced Qeihong's compromises ran the same specifications as the compromises he had been sent to audit.

Deeper in the Stack, the assessment facilities processed artifacts pulled from strata the mining operations had breached. Shumei followed every void-assessment procedure — documented each deviation, flagged each warning, filed each spectral anomaly through the proper channels. The system certified her for three additional artifacts per season. Section K, already highlighted. She processed them. The void in the eastern assessment chamber consumed light at a rate the maintenance logs recorded and no supervisor addressed. Corruption spread through repaired vault sections at four centimeters per cycle. A maintenance request was submitted. No one followed up. No one would. The institution did not die in crisis. It died at the speed of an unprocessed requisition, in a queue that would outlast the office that created it.

The Nightshelf consumed its own foundations with equivalent thoroughness.

The Firekeeper Doctrine — the pedagogical catechism undergirding institutional identity — had read, in the original formulation preserved in stratum 4 administrative ice: *What is shared is power multiplied.* Someone scored through it with deliberate heat-work and replaced it. The corrupted version — *What is hoarded is power preserved* — entered living practice as call-and-response, recited by students who did not believe it, taught by instructors who did not believe it, embedded in a pedagogy so structurally load-bearing that by the middle years of the war the original survived only in ice no one was reading. The corruption was architectural — woven into the catechism's rhythm so that the new words arrived on the same beat as the old, occupied the same silence after, produced the same nod of completion. The students finished the phrase automatically. The phrase finished them.

The Mask at Thrall Vavmastius — the Nightshelf's apparatus for selecting operatives through controlled grief-exposure — assessed candidates with specifications requiring grief in a precise range. A hatchling registering at zero was not rejected. The hatchling was watched — numbered, monitored, permitted to accumulate the losses that would bring the index to operational threshold. The system required its candidates to break in the correct way and called this patience. One candidate — identified only as Fourteen — sat down directly above evaluation channels saturated with ice-memory from depths the Mask's specifications did not include, and did not rise. The Mask classified the event as irrelevant. The channels beneath that seat carried impressions from strata predating any known habitation. Fourteen was not weak. Fourteen was receiving.

Crystal shipments logged as Aurora origin but bearing Nightshelf crystalline signatures indicate that someone operated a parallel economy outside both factions' chains of custody. The factor-of-three discrepancy — too large for opportunistic smuggling, too small for war-scale supply — points to systematic stockpiling by actors with access to both networks and allegiance to neither. This parallel economy operated concurrently with the Crystalline Spiral's deployment at Terrace Momiou, where an experimental fusion of aurora-light and captured flame broke strata sealed since before habitation. The Accord's formal apparatus continued holding votes while the corridors beneath it moved material that answered to no charter and no quorum. The Midnight Claws likely did not build this shadow economy. They inherited it, systematized it, defended it with the pragmatic violence that fills every space governance has abandoned.

---

Along the outer currents, the orca pods circled.

They had been circling since before the war. The Thaw-era scouts had glimpsed their shapes at the ice-edge and traded stories in whispers, and the whispers had been accurate in substance if not in implication — the pods were real, the pods were watching, and the pods were not waiting for permission. They were waiting for fragmentation.

At least two raids that penguin chronicles record as legendary acts of unprovoked aggression were retaliatory strikes. The ice preserves the expansion records: penguin factions, competing to outflank each other, pushed fishing operations and colony infrastructure onto hunting grounds the orca pods had held for generations. The raids followed the encroachment at intervals consistent with matriarchal deliberation — councils convened, pod resources allocated, strikes executed against the specific installations that had displaced orca hunting access. Penguin histories frame these as the savage opening movements of an existential threat. The ice frames them as what happens when you build a colony on someone else's grounds and are surprised when they come to feed.

The matriarchal command structure unified pod operations only when all pods hunted together — a constitutional threshold reserved for existential campaigns. This unification activated during exactly the windows when Aurora-Nightshelf conflict made coordinated penguin defense impossible. The correlation is too precise for coincidence and too consistent for luck. The orca did not need to understand penguin politics. They needed only to watch the patrols thin, the fire-barriers go unmaintained, the signal-towers stand dark because the colony that manned them was fighting the colony that supplied their fuel.

An elder named Keth'nal remembered when orcas owned the surface — had watched sovereignty contract across generations to ritual, then to pilgrimage, then to desperation dressed in liturgy. The Wake-Singers were the era's most disciplined institutional actors. Decades of blood-sacrifice, ritual accumulation, and expenditure of cultural capital directed toward a single operational objective: breach the fortified passages and descend. Not across the surface. Down. Their theology — the dead god sleeping in the deep, the blood that feeds the waking, the endless sea that yields when the count is met — produced an institutional program of extraordinary coherence aimed at an objective entirely outside the framework of penguin factional politics. While the factions fought over corridors and fire-core allocations, the orca pursued something that had nothing to do with corridors or fire-cores or the Flipper Accord or the silence law or any of the instruments the penguin world had built to manage its own disintegration. The penguin wars were, from the orca vantage, territorial noise that happened to weaken the defenses around the passages the Wake-Singers needed.

The Shelf Crushing Offense lasted forty-three days.

Fire-barriers cracked. Primary cores permanently lost. Sustained military operation against hardened positions — coordination, logistics, and command authority the penguin factions had not credited the pods with possessing. Two minor factions, Weal' Clique and Aureate — barely documented, almost footnotes — locked flippers in the dark as the walls came down around them. Whether this was cooperation or shared annihilation the accounts do not clarify, because the accounts were written by survivors who had already decided what the Offense meant, and what it meant depended on who was writing. Forty-three days. The penguin factions had been too occupied crediting each other with bad faith to credit the orca with anything at all.

The Offense broke the eastern defenses. The Wake-Singers poured through the gap.

They went down.

---

The deep had been responding before anyone agreed to notice.

Three days of melt without temperature change in the tunnels beneath Thrall Vavmastius — structural compression from below, not seasonal thaw. The ice wept before the Nightshelf acknowledged the Obelisk's rise, and the weeping was pressure from strata no drilling operation had reached, because the Nightshelf had stopped drilling at a certain depth and would not say why. The Obelisk — whale-bone that defied burial, that rose through layers of containment the Remembrancers had laid and relaid across generations — surfaced in the memorial corridor and wept brine. The memorial wept silence. Both were containment failures. Obscureth~ met them where they intersected, sharpened her chisel, and climbed the stairs. There is no other response to something that rises regardless.

At Terrace Momiou the Crystalline Spiral broke strata sealed since before habitation, and the impressions it released did not fade. Echoes fade. These persisted — carried through repaired vault sections alongside the corruption that spread at four centimeters per cycle, alongside the spectral manifestations that Shumei documented and filed and that the system absorbed without action. Years after the Spiral's deployment, Xouhoulong refused to witness one such manifestation — a spectral form of Selka — and requested that it be filed instead. The haunting made administrative. The evidence column left empty. The form completed.

At Lock-Camp the survey lines changed between observations. The chart was being rewritten by something beneath the ice that needed no codex, no faction, no legitimacy. A counting sequence emerged from the deep strata: thirteen, then twenty-one. Patterns matching impressions found in foundation ice at depth nine, pressed into layers predating any known civilization. The border colonies reported structured songs from below — not whale-song, not tectonic settling, not the ambient grinding of a berg in motion. Structured. Responsive. Whatever the Wake-Singers descended toward had been singing back before they arrived.

The Aurora Stack treated the deep as a resource to be administered. It filed assessments, submitted maintenance requests, documented spectral anomalies, and processed the documentation through channels designed for supply-chain management. The Nightshelf treated the deep as a secret to be contained. It buried the Obelisk, carved memorial gaps around what could not be named, stopped drilling at depths it would not discuss, and classified the containment failures alongside every other failure the silence law forbade acknowledging. The orca alone treated the deep as a destination — their entire theological apparatus oriented toward engagement with what lay below. And each posture constrained the others. The Nightshelf's concealment prevented Aurora from assessing the risk. Aurora's administrative approach prevented recognition of what the orca already understood. And the orca's descent removed from the surface the only faction willing to go where the sound was coming from.

The Wake-Singers did not come back up. Either they found the dead god or they did not. Either the waking commenced or it was always commencing. The theological apparatus that produced this descent — generations of ritual, blood-price, matriarchal patience — had been functional the entire time, and its product was permanent departure, which is an outcome no penguin framework can evaluate and no penguin historian can confirm was failure.

The counting continued. It was not geothermal settling.

---

The terminal council convened beneath clear aurora. Quorum met. Protocols observed.

Three accounts survive from three narrators, each describing the same session from an angle calibrated to absolve the narrator's faction. Cross-referenced against the ice, they describe a chamber in which every participant was so practiced at positioning that no one remained to govern. Seven fire-cores sat on a shelf at the back of the room, untouched. Warmth refused because accepting it meant conceding need, and need meant conceding leverage, and leverage was the only currency that still denominated in survival. A compromise leader — unnamed in every protagonist's account, unnarrated in every chronicle, furniture in the story of those who positioned while this figure worked — restored fire-core shipments at sixty percent of pre-war levels. Forty rotations to the Long Dark. Someone performed this arithmetic and called it success, because the alternative was performing the arithmetic and calling it what it was.

The Pinnacle vault held a harp with three fracture lines, its resonance patterns shifting, its filament count disputed by custodians who would permit no outside assessment because custody had become doctrine. The cracked Mirror at the eastern colonies bisected two populations with a literalism the record finds difficult to present with gravity. The Aurora Stack had governed itself to paralysis through the same consensus that had once governed it to prosperity, and the apparatus could not distinguish between the two states, because the apparatus had never been designed to ask what it was producing. Every audit filed. Every council convened. Every competent subordinate present and informed and unauthorized to intervene. The vulnerable walked into dark that was thoroughly documented.

The Nightshelf had consumed its practitioners with the same rigor that had once protected them. The Mask selected through calibrated grief. The silence law strangled internal reform. The corrupted catechism reproduced itself in every classroom, and the recitation outlasted conviction, because that is what catechisms are built to do. A woman named Silvsiaelrous — ice-reader of rare ability, keeper of fire-tea for tunnel-runners, speaker of dead miners' names into dark ice, wearer of a fire-core amulet that was always warm — was used by the Order until she broke. That is the Faction Wars compressed past the point where further compression is possible.

The Mask eventually chose its operative — Lucatransiius, who had signed a lie in ash decades before and been rewarded with thirty years of carrying the dead. Selected through accumulated grief-weight. Through the crack in the selection chamber someone watched: inherited memory or something worse, rising from the same strata Fourteen had been listening to when Fourteen sat down and did not rise.

The world beneath did not choose sides. It responded to pressure the way geology responds to pressure. The Spiral broke strata. The Obelisk rose. The corridors rewrote their own survey lines. And the maintenance request sat in a queue, and the queue outlasted the office, and the ice recorded it all with the fidelity of a medium that cannot decide what is worth preserving and so preserves everything.

---

What survived was small and unsponsored and carries the weight of the era more honestly than any chronicle written by the victors — of whom there were none.

Kael left fire-core offerings at memorial stones no one else visited, her entire bloodline dead in the galleries. There was no institutional framework for this grief, no protocol, no form. She carried warmth to cold stone because the dead were cold and she had warmth, and that arithmetic required no quorum and no consensus and no clear sky.

Mairae~ and Hollows conducted triage across impassable distances through letters the silence law constrained into code. Each read the other for symptoms neither could name directly. *The old trouble* — said twice, by both, as though the phrase itself were medicine. *I hear your voice when I read your letters. That is enough for now.* Care survived prohibition by reading between lines both writers had learned to inscribe, and the letters are the most honest documents the era produced, because they were written for no audience but each other, with no purpose but keeping someone alive one more rotation. The memorial Mairae~ did not visit. The sealed entrance someone stared at. Everything in those letters orbits an absence, and two people at the limit of what can be endured chose to remain legible to each other.

Valveus-morb~ named the dead he had watched die while he survived. The Obelisk carried kill-counts in whale-bone — orca accounting in a medium penguin culture could not read without assistance. Valveus-morb~ answered with witness-counts: testimony where ritual was expected, memory where theology required blood.

Alnae~ Oss fed truth to the thing that feeds on lies at Terrace Momiou and died in a subordinate clause of someone else's chronicle, though her trajectory deserves its own. Ice-burn navigation markers were found scorched into her flippers, their contents unrecorded because the chronicler either did not know or chose silence. Zheishongfeihua set two warm cups on a surface and said *If you're staying,* which asked for nothing except presence and received it.

A thirteenth segment was added to the Compass — navigation toward acknowledged costs rather than denied truths.

Obscureth~ sharpened her chisel. Houchaiqangwei's final dispatches require magnification — the script compressed so far it resembles geological strata more than language, legible only to those willing to bring their eyes close enough to read what a woman wrote when the ink ran low and the silence law ran high and the only rebellion available was to keep writing. The record survived. It is being read now, under conditions the writer could not have anticipated, by a reader she could not have imagined, in an archive whose two assistants have been reassigned and whose funding has been redirected to what the Council calls forward-facing initiatives.

The systems worked. They produced precisely what working systems produce when the conditions exceed the parameters the designers believed impossible and no mechanism exists to make the machinery stop. The war-weary survivors who walked into the Clever Ice Age carried not the memory of catastrophic failure but something the ice finds considerably harder to file: the knowledge that everything had functioned, that the procedures had been followed, that the quorum had been met, that the vote had been taken under clear sky, and that the clear sky had looked down on all of it and changed nothing.

The chisel always needs sharpening. The flipper sharpens it. The stone waits. The deep counts. The page turns.
