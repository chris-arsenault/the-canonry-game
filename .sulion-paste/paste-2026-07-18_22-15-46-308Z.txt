Yes, multi-epoch evolution is the better model, and it should replace the proposed single-pass rebuild now. Implementing a new single-pass generator first would likely mean rewriting it again once persistent geology is introduced.

The right model is not “run the whole generator four times.” It is “create crust once, then evolve persistent state through four configurable epochs.”

That distinction matters because rerunning today’s layers would erase history: lakes, flow, ice, warmth, and surfaces are currently cleared and recomputed. Realistic terrain comes from old structures surviving while later processes modify them.

This approach is well grounded. Procedural terrain work has successfully coupled tectonic uplift with stream-power erosion to produce watersheds, dendritic rivers, and ridges; landscape-evolution models evolve topography over time under changing tectonics, precipitation, and sea level rather than generating each independently. Multiple glacial cycles likewise leave materially different terrain than a single glacial stamp. Cordonnier et al., eSCAPE, multi-cycle glacial erosion.

The epoch model
Four epochs is a good initial authored history, but EpochCount and the epoch definitions must be configurable.

A useful starting history is:

Crust assembly Strong plate movement, rifting, subduction, initial mountain uplift, volcanism, warm climate, and large marine basins.

Orogeny and wet erosion Continued uplift, stronger precipitation, major river incision, sediment transport, coastline development, and established drainage basins.

Glacial maximum Colder climate and lower sea level; glaciers accumulate, flow, erode valleys, deposit moraines, redirect rivers, and create overdeepened basins. Aurora strongly accumulates in this epoch.

Retreat and present world Reduced tectonic activity, rising sea level, glacier retreat, lake formation, river recapture, delta construction, present sea ice, and final surface classification.

Each epoch would contain a small configurable number of surface-evolution substeps. Four epochs are narrative/geological eras, not merely four numerical iterations.

Persistent state would include:

plate identity, crust type, age, and motion;
bedrock elevation and erodibility;
sediment thickness;
fault, fracture, and tectonic strain history;
geothermal and volcanic history;
maximum and cumulative glacial erosion;
cumulative fluvial and coastal erosion;
old river valleys and abandoned channels;
persistent magic deposits and scars.
Derived state—ocean, temperature, rain, current flow routing, active glaciers, lakes, present rivers, and current sea ice—is rebuilt as necessary from that persistent history.

Memory should remain well inside the 4–8 GB target because epoch scratch arrays can be reused. We should not retain four complete copies of the world.

Hotspots and tectonics
You are correct that arbitrary hotspot placement will continue to feel fake.

One terminology distinction: mountain ridges are not necessarily volcanic sources. Physical heat should originate from:

convergent plate boundaries and volcanic arcs;
divergent boundaries and rifts;
transform/fault intersections;
rare mantle plumes within plates.
A fixed mantle plume beneath a moving plate naturally produces a chain of progressively older volcanic sites. We can model this without simulating deformable crust: preserve each plate’s motion vector, move the active intersection each epoch, and retain attenuating deposits from previous epochs.

That produces the “original hotspots spread out” effect you want. Boundary volcanism moves as plate boundaries evolve, while plume tracks cross plate interiors.

The current five forced peaks would be deleted entirely.

Magic should participate asymmetrically:

Fire runs every epoch after tectonics and fractures. It records active vents, extinct volcanic provinces, thermal veins, and old basalt fields. Its heat feeds that epoch’s climate and ice.
Aurora runs after glaciation in sufficiently cold epochs. Some charge remains trapped in persistent ice or mineral deposits; later thaw creates melt lines and exposed prisms.
Pressure runs after ocean connectivity and currents. It affects marine sediment, scars, and coastal energy. Ancient effects persist even if the coast subsequently moves.
Final magic surfaces are classified only after the last epoch; earlier epochs write history fields and persistent features rather than repeatedly replacing the visible surface.
Rain should be first-class
Yes. The present moisture approximation is insufficient for landscape evolution.

Each epoch should have a discrete precipitation layer driven by:

ocean and open-water fetch;
prevailing wind directions;
temperature;
elevation;
orographic lift;
rain shadow;
local geothermal thaw;
epoch-specific wetness.
Rain then drives runoff, river discharge, lake balance, snowfall, and glacial accumulation. Changing precipitation across epochs is an established landscape-evolution forcing, not decorative noise. eSCAPE’s model explicitly couples spatial and temporal precipitation with tectonics, erosion, deposition, and sea-level variation.

Glaciation should actually evolve terrain
Yes, but with a reduced-order model rather than a full ice-sheet PDE.

Within cold epochs:

Calculate accumulation and ablation from precipitation and temperature.
Route ice flux over the current topography.
Erode bedrock according to flux, slope, and substrate.
Widen and flatten occupied valleys.
Transport and deposit sediment at margins and termini.
Update elevation.
Recompute drainage after the terrain changes.
That gives us genuine glacial capture, U-valleys, hanging valleys, fjords, moraines, overdeepened lake basins, and rivers reorganized by ice. Large-scale glacial stream-power approaches are specifically intended to make this kind of coupled evolution computationally practical. OpenLEM glacial erosion study.

Coastal erosion is worth including now
It belongs in the epoch model because coastlines should be consequences of ocean history, not merely sea-level contours.

A reduced coastal process should use:

open-ocean fetch and wave exposure;
current direction;
pressure-magic amplification;
shoreline angle;
substrate resistance;
shelf slope;
sea-level change;
available sediment.
It should erode exposed headlands and cliffs, move sediment alongshore, and deposit it in protected bays, beaches, shelves, and river mouths. We do not need a detailed ocean-fluid solver. Reduced cellular coastal models already produce erosion, deposition, spits, cuspate forms, and changing shorelines from wave angle, fetch, sediment, and sea-level history. CEM2D, NEWTS.

Physical waves and currents remain the baseline. Pressure magic modifies that energy; it does not become the only reason coasts erode.

Create IceRemembers.WorldGen now because the epoch orchestrator has no sensible home in Seed.

Engine.WorldGen: physical map state, plate/crust algorithms, generic evolution layers.
IceRemembers.WorldGen: authored epoch schedule, magic processes, surface taxonomy compilation, and quality profiles.
IceRemembers.Seed: consumes the finished blueprint for entities, subdivisions, and placement.
Engine.Kernel: unchanged and unaware of epochs or microtriangles.
Add persistent-versus-derived field contracts, configurable epoch definitions, reusable scratch storage, and layer dependency/invalidation semantics. Move existing domain generation out of IceRemembers.Seed without preserving compatibility wrappers.

Commit and push this boundary independently.

Milestone 2: Crust and tectonic history
Delete the center-and-ring continental model.

Implement:

irregular plate/crust provinces across and beyond the padded crop;
continental/oceanic crust and plate motion;
convergent uplift, rifting, trenches, and island arcs;
moving plate boundaries across epochs;
fault and fracture history;
tectonically grounded geothermal systems and mantle-plume tracks;
sea-level calibration against configurable ocean targets.
Checkpoint views should expose plate IDs, crust type, boundary type, uplift history, and bedrock elevation.

Tests must cover plate spacing, crop independence, coast/landmass targets, movement across epochs, hotspot provenance, and rejection of the current radial layout.

Commit and push.

Milestone 3: Coupled epoch surface evolution
Implement the per-epoch sequence:

tectonic displacement and uplift;
ocean connectivity and broad current field;
physical geothermal plus fire history;
temperature and precipitation;
snow/ice mass balance and glacial flow;
aurora deposition;
drainage and runoff;
fluvial erosion and sediment transport;
pressure propagation;
coastal erosion and alongshore sediment movement;
glacial erosion and deposition;
ocean and drainage reconciliation after elevation changes.
Old one-shot erosion, fixed-fraction ice selection, and stamped glacial-landform behavior would be deleted.

Commit and push after the complete four-epoch physical history produces coherent continents, varied ice, distributed heat systems, evolved coasts, and inland basins.

Milestone 4: Final hydrology and river geography
On the final evolved topography:

replace index-biased flat routing;
calculate precipitation-derived discharge;
construct tributary trees and Strahler order;
retain a narrow flow spine;
widen channels laterally from discharge and valley geometry;
add floodplains, abandoned channels, distributaries, mouths, and deltas;
allow sufficiently large rivers to occupy much or all of a hex’s 24 triangles.
Add river_channel, floodplain, and delta surfaces alongside river_valley.

River-width scaling with drainage area is consistent with landscape-evolution work on lateral channel erosion and valley widening. Langston and Tucker.

Tests must include graph-renumbering and rotational-equivalence cases so storage order can never choose a physical direction again.

Commit and push.

Milestone 5: Final magic, surfaces, UI, and quality
Resolve active versus relict fire, aurora, and pressure features.
Classify final natural and magic surfaces once.
Render filled river channels and deltas rather than representing everything as a line.
Add inspection views for crust age, erosion history, sediment, maximum glaciation, active/relict faults, and magic age.
Keep distribution requirements configurable: ocean, land ice, sea ice, landmass sizes, coast complexity, hotspot spacing, channel coverage, direction balance, tributary density, wide-river prevalence, and delta prevalence.
Keep only structural truths hard: topology, routing, provenance, conservation, and deterministic behavior.
Run multi-seed fixed-corpus validation, then final make ci, commit, and push.

This revised plan supersedes the earlier single-pass proposal. It does not change frontier radius, entity density, the semantic map, simulation behavior, or kernel terrain contracts.
