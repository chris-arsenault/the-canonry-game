You’re right. I overextended from several confirmed defects into replacing the platform around them. The layer boundaries already give us the mechanism for controlled evolution; we should use them.

The revised rule is:

Keep every layer whose algorithm is fundamentally sound.
Improve inputs, state, validation, or iteration locally.
Add a layer when a genuinely new process is missing.
Rewrite only the small algorithms whose core premise is incompatible—currently the radial continent placement and index-based flat drainage/ice selection.
Inspect the output after each focused milestone before compounding more processes.
A multi-epoch runner should come after the individual layers produce good results. Repeating broken continent, ice, or drainage layers would only amplify their artifacts.

4. Add river extent, floodplains, and deltas
This is a genuinely missing process, so it deserves a new independent RiverChannelGeometryLayer.

It will consume the existing river spine, discharge, slope, valley shape, sediment, and coast data and produce:

lateral channel coverage;
banks and floodplains;
broad major-river channels;
river mouths;
distributaries and deltas.
The existing flow spine remains authoritative. The new layer expands physical expression around it.

Extend the existing blueprint/projection/UI and add river_channel, floodplain, and delta surfaces. A large river can then occupy many or all microtriangles in a hex without moving the simulation below hex scale.

5. Add missing rain, currents, and coastal erosion layers
Only after macro geography and drainage are demonstrably sound:

Add PrecipitationLayer, reusing moisture transport and rain-shadow calculations.
Add a reduced OceanCurrentLayer.
Add CoastalErosionLayer consuming ocean exposure, current, substrate, pressure energy, and sediment.
Feed precipitation into existing runoff and glacial mass balance.
Feed currents into existing temperature, sea ice, and coastal layers.
These are additions, not replacements.

6. Introduce configurable epochs by reusing the working layers
Once every individual process produces acceptable output, add an epoch loop around them.

The loop will repeatedly invoke the existing independent layers:

incremental tectonic uplift;
ocean/bathymetry/current reconciliation;
geothermal and fire;
temperature and precipitation;
land ice and ice flow;
aurora;
hydrology and fluvial erosion;
pressure and coastal erosion;
sediment/glacial deposition;
final derived-field reconciliation.
Required changes are primarily persistent history fields and an epoch-aware context. The physical algorithms remain the ones already tested in milestones 1–5.

FluvialErosionLayer, IceFlowLayer, GlacialLandformLayer, and the magic propagation layers are reused rather than replaced. Active fields may be recomputed each epoch while cumulative erosion, thermal, glacial, and magic history persists.

Four authored epochs will be the initial profile, not a hard-coded engine constant.

Moving domain generation from IceRemembers.Seed to IceRemembers.WorldGen remains the cleaner architecture, but it should be a mechanical milestone after output stabilizes. It will not be mixed into algorithm diagnosis.
