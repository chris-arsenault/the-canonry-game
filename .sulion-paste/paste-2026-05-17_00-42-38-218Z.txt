OK now I actually know what's in the code. The hierarchy already exists. I've been hallucinating gaps.

## What's actually there

**Relationship catalog** (`src/IceRemembers/IceRemembersSchema.cs:109-134` and definitions at `IceRemembers/IceRemembersDefinition.cs:112-127`) already includes the full affiliation web:

- `ControlledBy` — spatial→agency, exclusive, has maintenance (settlement under a faction)
- `HomeBase` — agency→spatial, exclusive (penguin/monster's home)
- `MemberOf` — agency→agency, has maintenance (membership in faction)
- `LedBy` — agency→agency, exclusive (faction leadership)
- `AlliedWith`, `RivalOf`, `ClaimedBy`, `NativeTo`, `PartOf` — all present

**Seed** (`src/IceRemembers.Seed/IceRemembersSeedBuilder.Entities.cs`) actually builds the hierarchy at startup:

- 4 factions: Council, Exchange, Claws, Wake-Singers (lines 378-381)
- Each faction `led_by` a specific named penguin (lines 138-143)
- Settlements `controlled_by` factions with varying strength: 1.0 for primary, 0.4/0.1 for partial/contested (lines 400-404)
- Penguins `member_of` factions with loyalty as strength, `home_base` at host settlements (lines 440-444)

**Generators maintain the hierarchy on new entities.** Every generator I looked at in `IceRemembersGeneratorDefinition*.cs` bundles affiliation edges into its output:

- When a generator makes a new settlement, it ships with `controlled_by` to a faction
- When it makes a new penguin, it ships with `home_base` and `member_of`
- When it makes a new monster, it ships with `home_base` to its lair region
- New entities come pre-wired into the hierarchy

**Gambits modify it.** `FactionChartersSettlement` writes `ControlledBy`. Trait/maintenance rules drift the strengths over time.

## What's actually missing

The simulation is producing the hierarchy. The projection and UI aren't reading it.

`EntityBrowser.cs` is a flat virtualized scrolling list of all active entities, filterable only by which generator made them or whether they're on-screen. There's no grouping by affiliation. No "top of hierarchy." No ranking.

`UiProjectionState` already maintains incoming/outgoing relationship indexes per entity (`_activeIncomingRelationshipsByEntity`, `_activeOutgoingRelationshipsByEntity`). The data is there. Nothing aggregates it into significance, ranks within altitude, or surfaces a stage.

That's the whole gap. The hierarchy lives in the graph. The UI shows entities as if it doesn't.

## What the change actually is

A projection extension and a UI surface. Specifically:

**One projection addition.** Add fields to `UiProjectionState` that derive, per entity, a graph-weight score from its existing relationship indexes — count and strength of incoming affiliation edges, weighted by the score of what they connect to, settled over a small fixed number of iterations. Recompute on the same version triggers that already drive `EntityBrowserVersion`. This is read-side code that walks data the projection already keeps.

**Altitude tagging on kinds.** In `IceRemembersDefinition.cs` (or alongside `IceRemembersKinds.cs`), tag each kind with an altitude label: Faction → realm, Settlement and Region → place, Penguin/Monster/Transport/Artifact/Orca → life. Single declarative pass. The projection groups by this tag when ranking.

**A new UI panel or a mode on `EntityBrowser`.** Show the top-N per altitude — the Stage. Could be a separate panel alongside the existing browser, or a mode toggle on the existing one. Existing `EntityBrowserVersion` change signal already invalidates it correctly. Reuses the same row scenes (`EntityRow.tscn`, `EntityKindHeader.tscn`).

**Hierarchy navigation in entity detail.** The `EntityCard` (or whatever surfaces entity detail) already reads relationships. Show the entity's hierarchy context — its parent (the faction it's `member_of`, the settlement it has `home_base` at), its siblings, its children (for a faction: its members and controlled settlements). Pure read across data already in the projection.

## What this does not require

- No new entity kinds. The 11 in `IceRemembersKinds.cs` are sufficient.
- No new relationship kinds. The 23 in `IceRemembersSchema.cs` cover the affiliation web.
- No new attributes. The per-kind health scalar isn't being repurposed; the new score is derived in the projection, not stored.
- No new gambits or generators. Content authoring round can continue as planned — broader affiliation coverage helps, but isn't gated on this work.
- No kernel changes. No new events. No new buffers.

## What this fixes

The "twenty diffuse settlements" complaint: settlements that have many strong `controlled_by`/`member_of`/`home_base`/`trade_route` edges around them rank high. Those become the Stage's Places. Settlements with few or weak edges fade into the chorus.

The "cities don't grow" complaint: a settlement does grow — its incoming affiliation edge count and strength increase as the simulation adds penguins with `home_base`, factions chartering it, etc. We're just not showing that growth. The projection makes the growth visible by ranking on it.

The "no reason to spotlight one over another" question: there is now. The Stage promotes the entities the graph itself says are central.

This is one projection's worth of code and one or two UI panels. The simulation work was already done.
